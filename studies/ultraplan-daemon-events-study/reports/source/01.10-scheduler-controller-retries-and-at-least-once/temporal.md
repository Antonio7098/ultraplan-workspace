## Summary

Temporal implements durable at-least-once execution with a layered scheduler/controller design: the matching service runs a sync-rendezvous matcher plus persistent backlog readers with fair dispatch, while the history service runs transfer/timer queue readers, a priority scheduler, and a rescheduler that re-queues failed executables. Desired-vs-observed reconciliation is explicit — history mutable-state plus timer tasks drive activity/workflow retries to convergence, and shard-ownership transfer re-reads uncommitted tasks from persistence. Retry ownership is split cleanly: the server owns durable retries (activity `RetryActivity`, workflow `GetRetryBackoffDuration`/`SetupNewWorkflowForRetryOrCron`, callback HSM backoff) computed from the user `RetryPolicy` with `MaximumAttempts`/`MaximumInterval`/`expirationTime` budgets, while RPC/persistence transient retries are ephemeral in-memory `ThrottleRetry` loops owned by `common/backoff`/`common/rpc`. Failure classification is explicit in `common/retrypolicy.IsRetryableFailure` (terminated/canceled never retryable; only start-to-close/heartbeat timeouts retryable; server/app non-retryable flags honored) plus a separate RPC taxonomy (`IsPersistenceTransientError`, `IsServiceTransientError`, `IsServiceHandlerRetryableError`). Backoff is exponential with a 20% jitter band (`ExponentialRetryPolicy.ComputeNextDelay`/`addJitter`, `FullJitter`/`Jitter` helpers). Duplicate execution is expected and made safe by history-level idempotency: `RecordActivityTaskStarted` dedups on `RequestId`, task tokens bind `ScheduledEventId+Attempt+Clock/Version`, and `StartWorkflowExecution`/`SignalWithStart` dedup on `RequestId` with `WorkflowIdReusePolicy`/`WorkflowIdConflictPolicy`. Scheduled (`TaskScheduled`/`BufferedStarts`) and started (`TaskStarted`/real dispatch) events are semantically distinct, with timeout/heartbeat-triggered re-dispatch reusing the same `ScheduledEventId` at a bumped `Attempt`.

## Rating

**9/10 — Mature, durable, observable, extensible, and proven under failure or scale.**

Rationale: durable retry state machines with explicit budgets, a published failure taxonomy with tests, centralized backoff/jitter implementations, fair/priority dispatch with rate limiting, crash recovery via persistence re-read plus rescheduler, and extensive duplicate-dispatch/idempotency tests across matching, history, and scheduler subsystems. Point deducted only because retry policy is intentionally stacked across two layers (durable server timers + ephemeral client RPC retries) requiring operators to reason about both, and fairness-weight tuning remains config-heavy.

## Answers to Dimension Questions

1. **Can the same logical step execute more than once?**
   Yes — at-least-once is the documented design. Poller crash or task expiry causes matching re-dispatch (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/task_validation.go:25-31`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/task_reader.go:255-265`); heartbeat/start-to-close timeout causes history `RetryActivity` to re-emit a transfer task with the same `ScheduledEventId` at a new `Attempt` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:6875-6971`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/timer_queue_active_task_executor.go:204-310`); shard-ownership loss causes uncommitted queue tasks to be re-read and re-executed (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/handler.go:2277-2288`). Safety comes from history dedup: started-dedup on `RequestId` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/api/recordactivitytaskstarted/api.go:150-171`), token attempt/clock/version checks (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/api/activity_util.go:58-80`), and idempotency guidance that activities must tolerate re-execution (heartbeat-gated progress via `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:2107-2132`).

2. **Is retry policy centralized or stacked across layers?**
   Stacked across two deliberate layers. Durable user-visible retries are centralized in one policy object (`RetryPolicy`: `MaximumAttempts`/`InitialInterval`/`MaximumInterval`/`BackoffCoefficient`/`NonRetryableErrorTypes`) interpreted by exactly two server state machines — activity (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:6875-6971`) and workflow (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/retry.go:31-112`) plus the callback HSM (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/hsm/callbacks/statemachine.go:210-259`). Underneath/around that sits an ephemeral RPC layer: `common/backoff` exponential/jitter primitives (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/common/backoff/retrypolicy.go:141-185`) composed into per-client policies in `common/util.go:163-288` (persistence, frontend, history, matching, long-poll, reschedule) and queue-executor `RetryPolicy()/IsRetryableError()` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/executable.go:472-623`). The layers do not share budgets: durable budget is attempts/expiry, ephemeral budget is in-memory attempts/expiry with no history.

3. **How does the scheduler recover capacity after crashes?**
   By re-reading durable state, not by lease fencing. Matching backlog is persisted (task expiry advances `readLevel` to avoid poison loops, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/task_reader.go:255-265`); unacked tasks remain visible to the new reader/forwarder after `RenewLease`/expiry (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/db.go:150-841`). History queues are persistence-backed: on shard-ownership transfer the new owner reloads transfer/timer tasks and the `Rescheduler` re-buffers failed executables (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/rescheduler.go:31-72`); in-memory-only state (poller counts, `waitingPollerCount`, rate-limiter tokens) is rebuilt from scratch. Rate limiting (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/scheduler_quotas.go:10-87`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/configs/quotas.go:10-96`) bounds the post-crash catch-up surge.

4. **Are scheduled and started events semantically distinct?**
   Yes, at three levels. Activity: `EVENT_TYPE_ACTIVITY_TASK_SCHEDULED` (intent + timeouts + retry policy, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/historybuilder/event_factory.go:230-258`) vs `EVENT_TYPE_ACTIVITY_TASK_STARTED` (lease to a specific worker attempt with `RequestId/Attempt/Identity`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/historybuilder/event_factory.go:260-281`) vs completed; the `StartedEventId==Empty` invariant (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:4334-4395`) plus transient-start deferral (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:4498-4530`) prove the split. Matching: `Offer` (intent) vs `poll`-rendezvous (dispatch). Schedules: `BufferedStarts{NominalTime,ActualTime}` intent (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/worker/scheduler/workflow.go:1354-1365`) vs `startWorkflow()` real start with fresh `RequestId` and `ScheduleActionResult{ScheduleTime,ActualTime}` delta (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/worker/scheduler/workflow.go:1475-1560`).

5. **Can priority/fairness decisions be tested deterministically?**
   Yes. Matching has `TestOrder` asserting poll order after scripted enqueue (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/matcher_data_test.go:546-561`), forward-timing tests keyed on `MaxWaitForPollerBeforeFwd` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/matcher_test.go:329-414`), poll-forward success/failed suites (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/matcher_data_test.go:563-607`), and fairness/rate-limit suites (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/fairness_util_test.go:11`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/ratelimit_manager_test.go:30-177`). History scheduler weights and reader priorities are config-injected (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/scheduler.go:105-166`), and scheduler overlap/backfill behavior has deterministic workflow-test coverage (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/worker/scheduler/workflow_test.go:404-1452`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/chasm/lib/scheduler/invoker_execute_task_test.go:838-860`).

6. **Crash after the external work succeeds but before acknowledgement. Is retry expected, and is the side effect safe?**
   Retry is expected; safety depends on the worker contract. If an activity worker performed the side effect but crashed before `RespondActivityTaskCompleted`, the `Started` heartbeat/start-to-close timer fires and history schedules a new attempt — the same logical step runs again. Temporal contains the damage via idempotent completion (stale token with mismatched `Attempt`/`Version` rejected, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/api/activity_util.go:58-80`; duplicate `RecordStarted` with same `RequestId` returns stored clock instead of double-starting, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/api/recordactivitytaskstarted/api.go:150-171`; second complete after terminal→`NotFound`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/history_engine_test.go:2915`). But the external side effect itself is only safe if the activity implementation is idempotent or heartbeat-gated — the server cannot undo worker-external writes. Workflow-start crashes are safer: same `RequestId` replays return the existing `RunId` without a second run (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/api/startworkflow/api.go:330-360`).

## Architectural Decisions

- **Durable timers own user retries; RPC retries stay ephemeral.** Activity/workflow retry progress is history events + timer tasks surviving crashes, while `ThrottleRetry` loops die with the process. Separates correctness (durable) from availability (ephemeral).
- **Budgets as attempts + time, not tokens.** No token bucket; `MaximumAttempts` + `MaximumInterval` + `expirationTime` bound infinite-retry footguns while preserving the common infinite-default (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/common/retrypolicy/retry_policy.go:66-100`).
- **Sync rendezvous first, persisted backlog second.** Matching prefers direct `taskC` handoff for ordering/latency and only spills to backlog readers/forwarding under load, gated by backlog age (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/matcher.go:108-128`).
- **Fairness as weighted round-robin + per-key rate limits.** Both matching (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/fair_task_reader.go:398-565`) and history (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/scheduler.go:95-185`) compose FIFO + execution-aware + weighted schedulers rather than strict priority, avoiding starvation.
- **Overlap policies as first-class schedule semantics.** SKIP/BUFFER_ONE/BUFFER_ALL/CANCEL_OTHER/TERMINATE_OTHER/ALLOW_ALL (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/worker/scheduler/buffer.go:30-113`) let schedule authors choose duplicate-start behavior instead of hardcoding dedup.

## Notable Patterns

- **RequestId as the universal idempotency key.** Activity starts, workflow starts, signals, and CHASM scheduler executes all key dedup off client-supplied `RequestId`, persisted in `RequestIds` map (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:2597-2631`).
- **Token binds dispatch to attempt generation.** Task token carries `scheduled_event_id/attempt/clock/version/stamp` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/proto/internal/temporal/server/api/token/v1/message.proto:39-55`) so late completions from a prior generation are rejected rather than double-applied.
- **Transient-start deferral.** Retryable activities record a transient started state without writing a history event until the attempt proves non-retryable (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl.go:4498-4530`), keeping history compact under flapping retries.
- **SDK-suggested delay, server-enforced budget.** `ApplicationFailureInfo.NextRetryDelay` can suggest per-attempt delay (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/retry.go:55-67`) but server clamps to max-interval and expiration.
- **Throttle-aware retry composition.** `ThrottleRetry` takes `max(data-plane backoff, throttle backoff)` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/common/backoff/retry.go:41-102`) so `ResourceExhausted` automatically slows all layers coherently.

## Tradeoffs

- **At-least-once + worker idempotency burden vs. simpler server.** Server guarantees delivery and dedup, but every activity author must make side effects idempotent; non-idempotent activities can double-apply on timeout races.
- **Infinite-retry default vs. operational surprise.** Default `MaximumAttempts=0` (infinite) with 1s→100x-max exponential (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/common/retrypolicy/retry_policy.go:66-81`) favors durability over fast failure; misconfigured policies can hold workflows open for a year (scheduler default catchup 365d, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/worker/scheduler/workflow.go:199-202`).
- **Sync-match latency vs. ordering strictness.** Blocking sync match when backlog is non-negligible (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/matcher.go:108-116`) preserves FIFO at the cost of added dispatch latency under backlog.
- **Weighted fairness complexity vs. head-of-line blocking.** Fair readers, pass/id levels, weight overrides, and per-key rate limits (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/matching/fair_task_reader.go:245-392`) prevent noisy-neighbor starvation but add substantial config surface (`MatchingEnableFairness`, `FairnessCounter`, weight overrides).
- **Two retry stacks to operate.** Durable and ephemeral retries have different knobs, metrics, and logs; an operator debugging a slow activity must check both the workflow retry backoff and the underlying RPC/queue throttling.

## Failure Modes / Edge Cases

- **Heartbeat loss → duplicate attempt.** Worker alive but heartbeats dropped past `HeartbeatTimeout` triggers a parallel attempt; both may run until one completes and the other's token goes stale.
- **Schedule-to-start vs start-to-close confusion.** Only start-to-close/heartbeat timeouts are retryable; schedule-to-start/schedule-to-close timeouts are terminal (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/common/retrypolicy/retry_policy.go:36-47`) — a permanently unpolled task queue fails fast rather than retrying forever.
- **Non-retryable type bypass via wrapping.** `IsRetryableFailure` inspects only the top-level failure; nested child-workflow/activity wrappers are ignored, so a non-retryable cause buried inside a retryable wrapper still retries.
- **Expiration vs max-interval clamp.** When `now+interval > expiration`, workflow retry yields `TIMEOUT` instead of a final attempt (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/retry.go:108-110`); activities with `NextRetryDelay` overriding max-interval (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/workflow/mutable_state_impl_restart_activity_test.go:235-253`) can delay past operator expectations.
- **Rescheduler/reader poison tasks.** Malformed or persistently failing executables cycle through `Rescheduler` + `HandleErr` attempt counting to DLQ/drop (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/temporal/service/history/queues/executable.go:590-623`); misconfigured DLQ leaves them looping with backoff.
- **Clock skew on expiry.** Task `ExpiryTime` and timer `fireTime+MinPrecision` comparisons assume reasonably synchronized clocks; skew can prematurely expire matching tasks or delay timer re-dispatch.
