## Summary

containerd has no single scheduler/controller. Scheduling and retry are fragmented across at least five independent loops, each owning its own budget and backoff: (1) the GC scheduler reconciles desired-vs-observed store pressure via pause/deletion/mutation thresholds (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:236`); (2) the restart monitor reconciles desired task state on a fixed 10s tick with per-namespace fan-out (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:90`); (3) the CRI `EventMonitor` backoff queue re-delivers failed TaskExit/SandboxExit events with per-key exponential backoff 1s→5min (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:34`); (4) the registry client stacks transport retries (max 5 attempts, 50ms fixed delay) plus status-code retries for 408/429/5xx (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:772`); (5) the transfer service bounds concurrency with weighted semaphores for uploads/downloads/unpacks (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/transfer.go:50`). Failure classification is ad-hoc via `errdefs` (`IsNotFound`/`IsAlreadyExists`/`IsUnavailable`) rather than a transient/permanent/conflict/throttle taxonomy. Duplicate execution is the norm (GC rerun, restart `NewTask`, event redelivery, registry re-fetch) and is tolerated through idempotent guards and content-addressed storage, not through exactly-once machinery. Crash recovery works for durable desired-state (restart labels, sandbox store) but the CRI backoff queue is in-memory and lost on daemon crash.

## Rating

**6/10** — Present but inconsistent and decentralized. The GC scheduler (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:236`), restart reconcile (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:102`), CRI backoff (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:126`), and registry retry (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:797`) each have a clear local model with tests (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler_test.go:30`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/runtime/restart/restart_test.go:109`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/integration/sandbox_run_linux_test.go:25`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver_test.go:839`), but there is no centralized retry policy, no shared jitter/backoff library, no priority/fairness model, and at-least-once is never stated as a contract — it must be inferred from redelivery loops plus idempotency guards.

## Answers to Dimension Questions

1. **Can the same logical step execute more than once? — Yes, routinely; safety comes from idempotent guards, not dedup.**
   - GC `GarbageCollect` reruns on every threshold breach and on failure reschedule (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:298`); collectors must tolerate repeat runs.
   - Restart `startChange.apply` can `NewTask`+`Start` again on the next 10s tick if the previous apply failed or raced (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/change.go:45`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:90`); no fencing token.
   - CRI exit events are redelivered via `Backoff`/`reBackOff` until `HandleEvent` succeeds (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:160`); handlers must be idempotent — they are, via `IsNotFound`-as-success (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/controller.go:193`).
   - Registry fetch walks multiple hosts/URLs and retries 5xx/transport errors (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:797`); duplicate GETs are safe because content is addressed by digest and writes are resumed by offset (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:191`).
2. **Is retry policy centralized or stacked across layers? — Stacked, with no shared policy object.**
   - Each layer owns its constants: GC `retryDelay` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:303`), CRI `1s→5min doubling` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:34`), registry `maxAttempts=5` + 50ms (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:772`), stop-container `maxRetries=3` quadratic (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/container_stop.go:87`), sandbox Wait doubling 128ms→4s (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/sandbox/proxy/controller.go:138`), content `16→2048` random (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:147`). A registry pull therefore traverses transport-retry × status-retry × host-fallback × content-resume — budgets multiply rather than compose.
3. **How does the scheduler recover capacity after crashes? — By re-deriving desired-vs-observed from durable stores on the next tick; in-flight backoff state is lost.**
   - Restart monitor is stateless: after crash it re-lists containers by `StatusLabel` and re-applies (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:131`); `CountLabel` persists the retry budget in container labels (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/runtime/restart/restart.go:52`).
   - Podsandbox `RecoverContainer` rebuilds `Wait` channels and re-arms `waitSandboxExit` only for still-running tasks; dead tasks become `NOTREADY` for kubelet-driven cleanup (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/recover.go:80`).
   - Transfer limiters are in-memory semaphores (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/transfer.go:50`) — capacity resets to full on restart; lease-based GC (`withLease`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/transfer.go:137`) lets leaked content expire rather than deadlock. CRI `backOff.queuePool` is a plain map (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:54`) with no persistence: events queued at crash time are dropped (gap).
4. **Are scheduled and started events semantically distinct? — Partially; only the GC path makes the distinction explicit.**
   - GC separates trigger (mutation/deletion event → `nextCollection` timer, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:266`) from execution (`GarbageCollect` call, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:298`) and completion broadcast (waiters, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:352`). No evidence found of distinct `Scheduled` vs `Started` event types on a bus — `wait()` waiters only observe completion or channel-close failure.
   - Restart has no schedule/start split: `monitor()` directly emits `startChange`/`stopChange` closures that execute inline (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:131`).
   - CRI conflates them further: `Backoff(key, evt)` enqueues the *work item itself*, and expiry immediately executes it (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:160`).
5. **Can priority/fairness decisions be tested deterministically? — No; there is no priority/fairness scheduler to test.**
   - GC ordering is threshold-arrival order with a single global timer — tests assert counts/delays, not ordering (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler_test.go:30`).
   - Restart applies all `changes` concurrently via `WaitGroup` with no ordering or quota (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:116`); transfer semaphores bound concurrency but not fairness (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/transfer.go:50`); registry host order is config order with last-host-only retry (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:863`). No evidence found of priority queues, weighted fair sharing, or deterministic dispatch-order tests.

## Architectural Decisions

- **Threshold-paced GC instead of periodic GC** (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:34`): pause-time ratio drives the interval, so GC pressure scales with observed cost rather than wall clock. Consequence: first-collection failure has no history, so it falls back to `scheduleDelay` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:303`).
- **Waiter coalescing on GC** (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:191`): N concurrent `ScheduleAndWait` callers share one collection. Consequence: failure closes all waiters indistinguishably — callers cannot tell transient from permanent.
- **Poll-based restart reconciliation over event-driven** (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:90`): 10s full re-list trades reaction latency for crash simplicity (no cursor to persist). Consequence: duplicate `NewTask` attempts under flapping tasks.
- **Per-sandboxID event backoff queues** (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:54`): isolates a poisoned sandbox from head-of-line blocking others; doubling 1s→5min bounds hot-loop risk. Consequence: queue is memory-only.
- **Last-host-only registry retry** (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:816`): preserves mirror fallback semantics — earlier hosts fail fast to the next mirror, only the terminal host spends the 5-attempt budget. Consequence: retry behavior depends on host list order, which is config-dependent.
- **errdefs-as-taxonomy** (e.g. `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/container_stop.go:127`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/fetcher.go:272`): `NotFound`/`AlreadyExists`/`Unavailable` double as both failure classes and idempotency signals. Consequence: no explicit throttle/conflict/permanent categories; 429 is retried (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:884`) but there is no token-bucket or `Retry-After` handling found.

## Notable Patterns

- **Desired-state-in-labels**: restart intent (`StatusLabel`, `PolicyLabel`, `CountLabel` in `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/runtime/restart/restart.go:44`) lives in the metadata store, so the controller is crash-safe without a WAL.
- **NotFound-means-done**: every destructive path (task delete, sandbox delete, stop) maps `IsNotFound` to success, making redelivery safe (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/controller.go:193`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/container_stop.go:127`).
- **Content-addressed dedup as idempotency**: pulls tolerate `IsAlreadyExists` and resume by digest+offset (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:191`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/pull.go:293`), so duplicate fetches converge.
- **Small bounded retry helpers at the edges**: `stopContainerRetryOnConnectionClosed` (3, quadratic, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/container_stop.go:87`), sandbox `Wait` (unbounded until ctx, doubling, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/sandbox/proxy/controller.go:138`), `OpenWriter` (unbounded until ctx, random doubling, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:149`).
- **Coalesced GC tests by timing, not mocks**: `scheduler_test.go` uses real timers and run-count assertions (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler_test.go:30`) — simple but timing-sensitive.

## Tradeoffs

- **Simplicity vs. predictability**: fixed 10s restart tick and 1s backoff ticker (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/monitor.go:90`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:262`) avoid timer wheels/heap complexity but add up to 10s restart latency and 1s redelivery granularity.
- **Local budgets vs. global overload**: per-layer retries (registry 5×, transport 50ms, content unbounded-on-reset) compose multiplicatively under a registry outage; no circuit breaker or global budget was found.
- **No jitter except two spots**: only `OpenWriter` (`rand.Intn`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:167`) and losetup (`rand.Intn(retry*10)`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/mount/losetup_linux.go:183`) randomize; CRI backoff, GC retry, and registry transport retry use fixed/doubling delays — thundering-herd risk after mass failure (e.g. registry 500 for many parallel pulls).
- **Concurrency without fairness**: transfer semaphores (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/transfer/local/transfer.go:56`) prevent OOM but a single large pull can starve others; no priority, preemption, or queue-depth metrics surfaced in the studied files.
- **In-memory backoff vs. durability**: CRI redelivery is cheap and fast but loses queued exit events on daemon crash; recovery (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/recover.go:51`) only heals tasks still present, not events already dequeued-and-failed.

## Failure Modes / Edge Cases

- **Crash after external work succeeds but before acknowledgement → retry is expected and mostly safe, with one exception.** Registry push/pull side effects are content-addressed and resume by digest/offset (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:191`); sandbox/task deletes tolerate repeats via `IsNotFound` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/controller.go:193`); GC reruns are read-mostly. The exception is restart `startChange.apply` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/restart/change.go:45`): it bumps `CountLabel`, kills, and creates a *new* task — a crash between `NewTask` and label persistence (or two concurrent ticks) can double-start or double-count; no fencing found.
- **GC waiter stampede on failure**: all `waiters` channels are closed on one failed collection (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:316`); N blocked `ScheduleAndWait` callers all observe `gc failed` and typically re-trigger, causing an immediate retry wave paced only by `retryDelay`.
- **Unbounded redelivery loops**: `OpenWriter` and sandbox `Wait` loop until `ctx.Done` with no attempt cap (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:149`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/sandbox/proxy/controller.go:138`); a permanently unavailable writer blocks `Transfer` for the context lifetime.
- **Poison-pill sandbox blocks its own queue but not others**: per-key queues (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:54`) isolate keys, yet within a key events are retried in order (`queue.events[i:]`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:167`) — one permanently failing event head-blocks later events for that sandbox.
- **Clock sensitivity**: GC `lastCollection.After(e.ts)` drops stale mutation events (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:267`); clock jumps can drop legitimate triggers or compress `retryDelay = next-last + 1s` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:306`).
- **Unbounded goroutine spawn on mutation bursts**: `mutationCallback` and `wait(trigger)` each `go func(){ s.eventC <- e }()` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:201`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:226`) — an unbuffered `eventC` under a slow `run()` loop spawns one goroutine per mutation.

## Future Considerations

- Persist CRI `backOff.queuePool` (or re-derive pending exit handling from task-store scan at startup, as `RecoverContainer` in `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/podsandbox/recover.go:51` already does for running tasks) so crash-time redeliveries survive daemon restarts.
- Introduce a shared retry helper (budget + exponential backoff + jitter + `Retry-After` + ctx) and migrate the five bespoke loops (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:303`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/events/events.go:254`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/remotes/docker/resolver.go:820`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/internal/cri/server/container_stop.go:87`, `studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/core/content/helpers.go:149`) to it; add `maxElapsedTime`/attempt caps where loops are currently unbounded.
- Add a `Scheduled` vs `Started` distinction (or at least attempt counters + last-error on waiters) so operators can tell queued from executing GC/restart work; expose queue depth and retry counts as metrics alongside existing `collectionCounter`/`gcTimeHist` (`studies/ultraplan-daemon-events-study/sources/containerd/plugins/gc/scheduler.go:302`).
- Document the at-least-once contract explicitly: which operations may run twice and which guards (errdefs mapping, digest dedup, lease expiry) make that safe — today it must be reverse-engineered from handlers.
