# Dimension 01.10: Scheduler Controller, Retries, and At-Least-Once Semantics

## Purpose

Study reconciliation-style scheduling, bounded concurrency, retry ownership, failure classification, and explicit at-least-once execution semantics.

## Steps

1. Identify the scheduler/controller loop and its desired-vs-observed model.
2. Inspect dispatch order, concurrency limits, quotas, and fairness.
3. Trace transient/permanent/conflict/throttle failure classification.
4. Find retry budgets, backoff, jitter, and the layer that owns retries.
5. Check duplicate-execution assumptions and idempotency requirements.

## Evidence to Capture

- Scheduler/controller loops
- Capacity and queue models
- Retry policies
- Failure taxonomies
- Backoff/jitter code
- Tests for duplicate starts and dispatch ordering

## Questions

1. Can the same logical step execute more than once?
2. Is retry policy centralized or stacked across layers?
3. How does the scheduler recover capacity after crashes?
4. Are scheduled and started events semantically distinct?
5. Can priority/fairness decisions be tested deterministically?

## Rating

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 1-3 | Absent, implicit, ad-hoc, or unsafe |
| 4-6 | Present but inconsistent, weakly documented, or fragile |
| 7-8 | Clear model with tests, explicit interfaces, and operational safeguards |
| 9-10 | Mature, durable, observable, extensible, and proven under failure or scale |

> Crash after the external work succeeds but before acknowledgement. Is retry expected, and is the side effect safe?
