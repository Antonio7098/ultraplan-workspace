# Dimension 01.04: Leases, Heartbeats, Fencing, and Stale-Worker Rejection

## Purpose

Study ownership leases and fencing mechanisms that prevent expired or superseded workers from mutating authoritative state.

## Steps

1. Identify how execution ownership is claimed and renewed.
2. Inspect heartbeat cadence, expiry rules, and clock assumptions.
3. Find fencing generations, epochs, revisions, or compare-and-swap ownership tokens.
4. Trace every authoritative mutation after ownership loss.
5. Check takeover and late-result behavior.

## Evidence to Capture

- Lease/lock schemas
- Heartbeat loops
- Generation or epoch fields
- Conditional updates
- Takeover/reconciliation paths
- Tests for stale writers

## Questions

1. Can two workers believe they own the same work?
2. Does lease expiry alone prevent stale writes?
3. Is fencing checked by product-state and artifact mutations, not only event writes?
4. How is process identity distinguished from reusable PIDs?
5. What happens to a late completion from a superseded attempt?
