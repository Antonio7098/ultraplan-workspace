# Dimension 01.07: Event Delivery, Backpressure, and Retention Tiers

## Purpose

Study how systems protect durable lifecycle facts while bounding high-volume progress, logs, token/output deltas, and slow consumers.

## Steps

1. Classify events by durability and retention requirements.
2. Inspect buffering, batching, fan-out, and slow-subscriber behavior.
3. Find policies for dropping, coalescing, or sampling high-volume events.
4. Check whether durable event writes block provider/runtime hot paths.
5. Inspect retention, compaction, pruning, and replay limits.

## Evidence to Capture

- Buffers and queues
- Batch writers
- Subscriber registries
- Drop/coalesce policies
- Retention/cleanup jobs
- Metrics for lag, queue depth, or WAL/log growth

## Questions

1. Which events must never be dropped?
2. Which events are ephemeral or short-lived?
3. Can a slow UI stall execution?
4. How are lifecycle and terminal facts flushed under pressure?
5. What bounds storage and in-memory growth?
