# 01.15 Executor Boundaries and Remote Readiness

Roadmap priority 15. Covers Phases 17 and 18 last, preserving Aren's rule that broader and distributed execution must be justified by real use.

## Purpose

Compare narrow Go executor boundaries that preserve type-specific success, cancellation, output, cleanup, artifact, and identity semantics while allowing later local or remote implementations without pretending they are uniform.

## Steps

- Identify the stable contract between scheduling or lifecycle ownership and a concrete executor.
- Trace identity and cancellation across process, container, node, or worker boundaries.
- Inspect setup, acknowledgement, streaming, artifacts, terminal reporting, cleanup, and lost-worker reconciliation.
- Check leases, heartbeats, fencing, duplicate dispatch, stale completion, and idempotency where remote work exists.
- Record which remote mechanisms solve demonstrated local constraints and which would be premature for Aren.

## Questions

- What remains common across executor types, and what stays deliberately type-specific?
- When is submitted work durably accepted, and how can a caller know?
- How are stale workers prevented from publishing authoritative completion?
- Which distributed mechanisms should Aren explicitly avoid until a measured requirement appears?
