## Executive Summary

All three systems bound hot resources explicitly, but the *shape* of the bound reflects product constraints: a broker (nats-server), a scheduler/orchestrator (nomad), and a model-runtime (ollama) each solve a different scarcity. nats-server is strongest on per-client byte isolation — `MAX_PENDING_SIZE 64 MiB` (`server/const.go:101`) with immediate revert on breach (`server/client.go:2598-2614`) and hard disconnect as overload primitive — plus a dual-track JetStream ledger (`memReserved/storeReserved` vs `memUsed/storeUsed` in `server/jetstream.go:115-118,2291-2299`) with periodic store-scan reconciliation. Nomad is strongest on disk and CPU reservations: per-node `AllocsFit` superset checks (`nomad/structs/funcs.go:142-198`) and client-side GC with three triggered thresholds (80% disk, 70% inode, 50 allocs in `client/gc.go:120-169`) protect the finite node, while pipeline backpressure (`nomad/plan_apply.go:100-106`) overlaps Raft latency. Ollama is strongest on pre-flight VRAM discipline: predicted (`llm/llama_server.go:2676-2698`) then verified via log-parsed `memoryParsingWriter` (`llm/llama_server.go:2700-2850`) with an explicit 80% free-memory headroom, serialized `activeLoading` (`server/sched.go:69-74`), per-runner `semaphore.Weighted(numParallel)` (`llm/llama_server.go:960`), and bounded 8 MiB stream scanner (`llm/server.go:160-162`).

Convergence is real: every system uses channel-capacity as the first backpressure primitive (nats-server `diskIOSemaphore` `server/dios.go:27-33`, nomad `EvalBroker`/`PlanQueue`/`ConnPool` in `nomad/eval_broker.go:53`, `nomad/plan_queue.go:33`, `helper/pool/pool.go:180`, ollama `pendingReqCh` `server/sched.go:91-96`), distinguishes reservation from usage, and guarantees release on failure/cancellation (nats `pb -=` revert `server/client.go:1921`, nomad `Nack`/`Ack` `nomad/eval_broker.go:599-674`, ollama `refCount` via `finishedReqCh` `server/sched.go:487-491`). Divergence is driven by operational context, not quality alone: nats-server can afford to kill slow consumers; nomad cannot kill evaluations and thus needs heap-based staging and deduplication; ollama cannot afford to spawn-then-crash OOM and thus needs predictive admission plus one-shot retry.

The shared gap is striking: all three score 6/10 because none implements process-wide memory-pressure shedding before OOM, none wires actionable metrics with alerting thresholds to admission, none enforces per-workspace or per-tenant fairness by default, and none ships saturation/soak benchmarks that assert bounded heap, queue depth, and retained memory under steady-state, burst, and long-lived workloads. For Aren — a per-run runner that must also protect workspace-wide and process-wide limits and prepare Phase 13 governance — the lesson is that explicit ledger + bounded queues are necessary but insufficient without soft degradation, quota scoping, and measured soak coverage.

## Core Thesis

Effective bounded-work governance requires five primitives kept together — an explicit admission gate, a reservation ledger, predicted→actual reconciliation, guaranteed release on failure/cancellation, and load-shedding with a deadline — and most production Go systems implement the first four in at least one subsystem while deferring the fifth until visible failure. The comparative evidence shows that the failure to complete the loop (no pre-OOM shedding, unbounded auxiliary queues, unbounded caches, and benchmark-free overload) is not an oversight but a deliberate throughput/observability tradeoff: internal control planes are left unbounded to avoid deadlocks (`server/ipqueue.go:87` without `ipqLimitByLen`), broker heaps are left uncapped to avoid rejecting work that Raft could absorb (`nomad/eval_broker.go:53`), and cache maps are left unbounded for hot-path simplicity (`server/model_inference_cache.go:19-23`). Aren should copy the converged discipline (channel caps, dual-track ledger, single-flight/stall channels, semaphore pools, optimistic-then-revert accounting) but must close the loop where these systems do not: define per-run, per-workspace, and process-wide budgets; shed softly before hard limits; expire retained history; and prove boundedness with hardware-pinned saturation tests.

## Approach Models

Three distinct archetypes emerge, each valid for its product shape:

**1. Hard-Cap Broker (nats-server).** Every routable unit (client, stream, store) gets an explicit byte/count limit enforced at enqueue. The overload response is binary: micro-stall (2-5 ms, total 10 ms at `server/client.go:125-127,3711-3749`) then hard disconnect (`SlowConsumerPendingBytes` `server/client.go:2614`, `SlowConsumerWriteDeadline` `server/client.go:1965`). JetStream adds a reservation ledger at create time (`reserveStreamResources` `server/jetstream.go:2718-2723`) and an actual-usage ledger updated atomically (`updateUsage` `server/jetstream.go:2291-2299`) with lazy reconciliation (`checkAndSyncUsage` `server/jetstream.go:2228-2272`). Disk I/O is separately semaphore-bounded (`server/dios.go:21-85`). Best fit when clients are cheap to reconnect and isolation must be deterministic. Copies well to Aren's per-run sandbox byte buffers and subprocess I/O.

**2. Staged Orchestrator (nomad).** Admission is a multi-queue pipeline: `EvalBroker` (ready/pending/unack/timeWait `nomad/eval_broker.go:53-122`), `BlockedEvals` decoupling via 8096 buffer (`nomad/blocked_evals.go:17-21`), `PlanQueue` heap (`nomad/plan_queue.go:33-40`) feeding a 6-8 deep Raft pipeline (`nomad/plan_apply.go:100-106`). Fitting is centralized (`AllocsFit` `nomad/structs/funcs.go:142-232`). Parallelism is capped by `EvaluatePool` workers `NumCPU/2` (`nomad/plan_apply_pool.go:11-26`) and GC semaphore `ParallelDestroys=2` (`client/gc.go:54-55`). Best fit when work is long-lived, re-queued, and Raft provides durability — abandoning work is costlier than queuing it. Copies well to Aren's run lifecycle (enqueue → block on capacity → plan → apply) and retention bounds.

**3. Predictive Model Runtime (ollama).** The scheduler owns all state via four `MaxQueue=512` channels (`server/sched.go:91-96`) and fast-fails on full (`ErrMaxQueue` `server/sched.go:88,204-208`). The scarce resource (VRAM) is not countable by the Go heap, so ollama predicts (`PredictServerVRAM` `llm/llama_server.go:2676-2698`) against `freeMemory*80/100`, then corrects by parsing subprocess logs (`memoryParsingWriter` `llm/llama_server.go:2700-2850`) and waiting for 75% VRAM reclamation (`waitForVRAMRecovery` `server/sched.go:1441-1498`). Loading is serialized (`activeLoading` `server/sched.go:69-74`), per-runner concurrency is semaphore-bounded (`llm/llama_server.go:960`), and overload is corrected via bounded OOM retry that steps `num_ctx` 32768→4096 (`server/sched.go:649-670`). Best fit when the resource is externally managed (GPU driver) and spawn failure is user-visible. Copies well to Aren's model residency and bounded inference/stream buffers.

**What converges:** all three bound *some* queue, *some* pool, and *some* retained history; all reconcile estimate vs actual somewhere; all release on failure.

**Why they diverge:** nats-server optimizes for tail latency under fan-in (kill slow, stall fast); nomad optimizes for throughput under Raft latency (pipeline and coalesce); ollama optimizes for stability under driver lag (predict conservatively, serialize, retry once).

## Pattern Catalog

**1. Channel-capacity as admission gate (fast-fail, not queue-with-timeout).**
Solves unbounded heap growth on burst. nats-server `diskIOSemaphore{ch chan struct{}}` `server/dios.go:27-33`, nomad `destroyCh` `client/gc.go:54-55` and `planIndexCh chan uint64` `nomad/plan_apply.go:100`, ollama `pendingReqCh = make(chan *LlmRequest, maxQueue)` `server/sched.go:91-96` with `select { default: ErrMaxQueue }` `server/sched.go:204-208`. Works because Go channel capacity is visible and lock-free. Copy when the producer can retry (API, transfer, GC). Overkill when the queue is an internal control plane that must never reject — then pair with `limitByLen/Size` but define the shed policy (nats-server `server/ipqueue.go:26-86` does not by default, which is the gap).

**2. Dual-track reservation ledger (reserved vs used) with scan-and-repair.**
Solves admission before data exists and drift under concurrency. nats-server `memReserved/storeReserved` vs `memUsed/storeUsed` `server/jetstream.go:115-118,2291-2299`, corrected by `checkAndSyncUsage` store scans `server/jetstream.go:2228-2272`; nomad `AllocsFit` superset check `nomad/structs/funcs.go:142-198` with snapshot invalidation `nomad/plan_apply.go:157-163`; ollama `PredictServerVRAM` vs parsed `memTotal/memGPU` `llm/llama_server.go:2712-2850` with `updateFreeSpace` `server/sched.go:1295-1335` and `min(accountedFree, systemFree)` `llm/llama_server.go:2591-2632`. Works because creation-time admission must not wait for usage. Copy for any bounded resource where "ask" and "actual" differ (disk bytes, VRAM, tokens). Risk: oversized reservation blocks admission while actual is low (nats-server `MaxBytes=1TB` fan-out `server/jetstream.go:2678`) — mitigate with per-stream `MaxBytesRequired` and periodic defrag.

**3. Optimistic accounting with immediate revert on breach.**
Solves TOCTOU between check and enqueue. nats-server `c.out.pb += len(data)` then `if pb > mp { pb -= len(data); slowConsumer }` `server/client.go:2598-2614` (same in `server/ipqueue.go:153-177` `pushMany`). Verified by `TestPBNotIncreasedOnMaxPending`. Works because the lock is already held and rollback is cheap. Copy for any `pendingBytes`-style gauge. Risk: assumes caller holds `c.mu` — document the invariant or make the method own the lock.

**4. Stall channel as binary backpressure signal (75% gate + 2-5 ms budget).**
Solves fan-in burst without global coordination. nats-server creates `stc = make(chan struct{})` at `pb > 75% mp` `server/client.go:2622` and producers `select { case <-stc; case <-timer }` `server/client.go:3743-3745`, closing when `pb < 75%` `server/client.go:1934`. `stalls` counter `server/server.go:424,3731` exposes saturation. Copy for fan-out to a single slow consumer. Avoid as global throttle — per-producer budget `stalledWait` `server/client.go:3711-3749` does not bound aggregate stall; pair with per-account cap if needed.

**5. Coalescing channel (size 1) to collapse bursts.**
Solves thundering herd on internal signals. nomad `waiting[sched] chan struct{1}` `nomad/eval_broker.go:373-377`, `PlanQueue.waitCh` `nomad/plan_queue.go:122-125`, `delayedEvalsUpdateCh` `nomad/eval_broker.go:288-291`, `triggerCh` `client/gc.go:110-114`; ollama `activeLoading` serialization enforces the same coalescing at the model layer `server/sched.go:517-528`. Works because one wakeup per batch is sufficient. Copy for any "something changed" signal. Never use for work items themselves — those need counted channels.

**6. Single-flight + semaphore-per-resource (Dial/Load/Runner).**
Solves duplicate spawn and SYN/placement storms. nomad `limiter map[string]chan struct{}` dial `helper/pool/pool.go:317-385` with per-IP `connlimit` `nomad/rpc.go:51-63`; ollama `activeLoading` single slot `server/sched.go:69-74` + `inferenceModelCache.loads.Do` singleflight `server/model_inference_cache.go:66-86` + per-runner `semaphore.Weighted(numParallel)` `llm/llama_server.go:960`. Works because one leader does the expensive operation while followers wait on close. Copy for model loads, TLS handshakes, and connection establishment. Pair with bounded retry — ollama's single `oomRetryAttempted` `server/sched.go:38-40` prevents infinite evict loops.

**7. Predict → Spawn → Parse → Correct loop with headroom and step-down.**
Solves external resource (GPU) that Go cannot count. ollama predicts with conservative KV formula `llm/llama_server.go:2676-2698`, gates at `free*80/100` and steps batch `2048→1024→512` `server/sched.go:828-834`, captures `using device X - Y MiB free` via `memoryParsingWriter`, `server/llm/llama_server.go:2733`, and absorbs driver lag with 5s 75% reclamation wait `server/sched.go:1441-1498`. nats-server does the same for disk (`diskAvailable*75%` `server/jetstream.go:2787`) but only at startup. Works because the subprocess is the source of truth. Copy for subprocesses, devices, and cgroups where `go/runtime` cannot see pressure. Overkill if the resource is heap — use `runtime.MemStats`/`GOMEMLIMIT` polling instead.

**8. Bounded history via ring/capped map/TTL.**
Solves retention growth over long-lived sessions. nats-server `closedRingBuffer MaxClosedClients 10k` `server/server.go:214,896`, `server/const.go:192`; nomad `enqueuedTime/dequeuedTime` truncation at 10k `nomad/eval_broker.go:337`, `pruneInterval 5m / pruneThreshold 15m` for `unblockIndexes` `nomad/blocked_evals.go:24-28`, `helper/users/cache.go:15-17` TTL 1h; ollama only `StatusWriter 8KiB` `llm/status.go:18` and otherwise unbounded caches — the negative example. Works because monitoring is the only consumer of history. Copy with explicit cap *and* TTL; truncation below reminds that dropping samples biases tail metrics.

**9. Acquisition bracketing with guaranteed release (semaphore + refCount).**
Solves leak on failure/cancel. nomad `destroyCh` acquire before `Destroy` and release after `client/gc.go:83,175-196` with `shutdownCh` guard, `refCount`/`shouldClose` `helper/pool/pool.go:73-79`; ollama `refCount` inc in `useLoadedRunner` and dec on `pending.ctx.Done()` via `finishedReqCh` `server/sched.go:487-491,1338-1352`, `expiredCh` retry when `refCount>0` `server/sched.go:418-424`; nats-server `dios.acquire/release` `server/dios.go:52-81` and `nbPoolGet/Put` tiers `server/client.go:365-423`. Works because the acquire and release are syntactically adjacent with defer/select. Copy with `defer` or `ctx.Done()` tie-off; never tie release to wall-clock alone.

## Key Differences

**Overload primitive:** nats-server disconnects (`SlowConsumerPendingBytes/WriteDeadline` `server/client.go:2614,1965`) — cheap to reconnect, isolation is per-connection. Nomad never disconnects evaluations — it Nacks, re-queues, and blocks indefinitely (`nomad/eval_broker.go:678-737`, `nomad/blocked_evals.go:36-78`), isolating by priority and per-job serialization (`nomad/eval_broker.go:320-349`). Ollama fast-fails the queue (`ErrMaxQueue` `server/sched.go:88`) but evicts models after (`findRunnerToUnload` `server/sched.go:265-268`). For Aren, the choice maps to SLO: disconnect when the client is external and retryable; Nack/block when the work is durable and re-planable; fast-fail when the resource is VRAM and retry is user-visible.

**Queue boundedness defaults:** ollama bounds every scheduler channel at 512 (`server/sched.go:91-96`). nats-server bounds only the edges (client pending `server/const.go:101`, IOSemaphore `server/dios.go:21`, `ipQueue` *capability* `server/ipqueue.go:75-82`) but instantiates most `ipQueues` unbounded (`server/server.go:372`, `server/sendq.go:28`, `server/events.go:133`). Nomad bounds the transport (ConnPool `helper/pool/pool.go:180`, pipeline depth `nomad/plan_apply.go:105`) but not the work heaps (`EvalBroker`/`PlanQueue`). The lesson: "capability to bound ≠ bound by default" — Aren must set caps at construction, not leave them optional.

**Memory-pressure signal:** nats-server uses a one-shot `GOMEMLIMIT`/`sysmem` heuristic at startup (`server/jetstream.go:2795-2801`) and never polls; nomad polls disk/inode on a timer (`client/gc.go:120`) and truncates metric maps (`nomad/eval_broker.go:337`) but never heap; ollama polls indirectly via predicted VRAM vs driver-reported free (`server/sched.go:536-570`) with batch step-down — the most responsive of the three outside the Go heap. None polls `runtime.MemStats` continuously or wires it to shedding.

**Reconciliation window:** nats-server corrects on demand plus 1500 ms tick (`server/jetstream.go:2316`) and 250 ms coalesce (`server/jetstream.go:2334`); nomad corrects via Raft apply and `RefreshIndex` retry (`nomad/plan_apply.go:709-718`); ollama corrects after each load via VRAM recovery wait up to 5s (`server/sched.go:1441-1498`). The tradeoff is latency vs staleness: shorter windows admit less but reject more accurately; longer windows batch but allow transient overcommit.

**Per-tenant isolation:** nats-server has per-account limits in JWT (`server/accounts.go:3502`) but no per-account pending cap — `N*64 MiB` fan-in is possible. Nomad has quotas (`nomad/structs/eval.go:200`, `nomad/blocked_evals.go:371`) but opt-in. Ollama has no workspace scoping (`OLLAMA_MODELS` global `envconfig/config.go:113`). All three demonstrate that without opt-out defaults, one noisy tenant can monopolize workers, buffers, or disk.

**Benchmark fidelity:** nats-server runs throughput micro-benches (`server/core_benchmarks_test.go:32`, `server/filestore_test.go:8623`) ignoring slow-consumer errors; nomad runs scheduler micro-benchmarks (`scheduler/benchmarks/benchmarks_test.go:74`) without retained-heap assertions; ollama ships an external `cmd/bench` tool (`cmd/bench/bench.go:519`) with benchstat but no checked-in soak. None asserts p95 queue time, heap after steady state, burst recovery, or 24h retention growth.

## Tradeoffs

**Hard disconnect vs soft shed.** Benefit: deterministic OOM isolation (nats-server `server/client.go:2603`). Cost: thundering-herd reconnect and lost ordering. Best-fit: edge broker. Alternative: queue deadline + 503 with `Retry-After` (missing in nats-server JetStream API, suggested in ollama future). Failure mode: slow-consumer kill with `pb` revert assumes lock discipline; missing lock causes under/over count.

**Reserved MaxBytes vs actual Bytes (nats-server `server/jetstream.go:2678`).** Benefit: prevents overcommit at create time. Cost: many empty streams with large `MaxBytes` block admission. Best-fit: storage. Alternative: overcommit with background reclamation (nomad `AllocsFit` does this by checking actual). Failure mode: reservation leak that keeps `memReserved` positive while `memUsed` diverges waits up to minutes for `checkAndSyncUsage`.

**Unbounded internal queues for throughput vs bounded for safety.** Benefit: no spurious 409s under spike (nats-server `jsAPIRoutedReqs`, nomad `EvalBroker`). Cost: heap OOM under sustained flood (nomad attacker can grow `ready+pending` indefinitely; nats-server API flood grows without `errIPQLenLimitReached`). Alternative: `ipqLimitByLen(10000)` plus `ipQueuesz` alerts (`server/monitor.go:1202`).

**Static 75% sizing vs adaptive polling.** Benefit: cheap, prevents unbounded defaults (nats-server `sysmem*3/4` `server/jetstream.go:2801`, ollama `free*80/100` `server/sched.go:536`). Cost: does not track cgroup updates or runtime free-space. Alternative: 30s `diskAvailable` and `sysmem` poll with `JSStorageResourcesExceeded` advisory early.

**Single load serialization vs parallel loads.** Benefit: prevents VRAM fragmentation (ollama `activeLoading`). Cost: tail latency on burst of distinct models. Alternative: partitioned `activeLoading` per device or per-model sharding.

**Per-runner semaphore vs global pool.** Benefit: per-model isolation (ollama `sem` `llm/llama_server.go:960`, nats-server per-client). Cost: no global concurrency cap allows `MaxRunners*NumParallel = 36` concurrent generations. Alternative: global `OLLAMA_MAX_CONCURRENT_GENERATIONS` process-wide semaphore layered over per-runner.

**Conservative overestimate vs accurate prediction (ollama KV formula).** Benefit: stability; avoids contention. Cost: throttles small models unnecessarily, still misses driver lag. Alternative: learned calibration per GPU/arch with `HeadCountKVMin` mapping validated against quantized backends.

## Decision Guide

**Choose per-client hard cap + micro-stall when** the producer is external, retryable, and total clients * cap fits in headroom (broker, gateway, fan-out). Configure stall at 70-80% headroom (nats-server 75% `server/client.go:2622`) with 2-10 ms budget and expose `stalls`/`slowConsumers` gauges (`server/server.go:412-433`, `server/monitor.go:1235`). Set per-account aggregate cap if `N*cap` exceeds host memory.

**Choose predictive admission + serialized spawn when** the resource is externally managed and spawn failure is user-visible (GPU, subprocess, device). Predict conservatively, gate at 75-80% free (`server/sched.go:536-570`), parse actual from child logs or driver, min with system free (`llm/llama_server.go:2591-2632`), and retry once with step-down (`server/sched.go:649-670`). Never predict without reconciling within one load.

**Choose staged heap + pipeline when** work is durable, ordered, and Raft- or WAL-backed. Keep one ready slot per logical key (`jobID` in `nomad/eval_broker.go:327`), heap pending by priority+index, pipeline 6-8 deep (`nomad/plan_apply.go:105`) with snapshot invalidation. Expect higher rejection under staleness — budget `RefreshIndex` retries.

**Shared rule:** bound every queue, pool, and cache at construction. If a component uses `ipQueue`, `PendingEvaluations`, or `map[digest]Entry`, give it `maxLen`/`maxSize`/`maxEntries` or TTL at birth. nats-server `server/ipqueue.go:26-86`, nomad `nomad/eval_broker.go:53`, and ollama `server/model_inference_cache.go:19` all show that optional limits stay unset.

**For Aren specifically:** treat Run as nats-server client (per-run `MaxPending` bytes, per-run `sem` slots, buffered stdout caps), Workspace as nomad node (sum of runs ≤ workspace quota via `AllocsFit`-style check, GC by disk/inode/alloc-count), and Process as ollama server (global VRAM/disk/model residency ledger with predict→verify and queue depth 512-like cap). Wire `GOMEMLIMIT` and `cgroup` pressure to a soft-shed tier before hard reject, because no studied source does this today.

## Practical Tips

**Patterns to copy now:**

- Make `maxPending` per-run explicit and revert on breach. Mirror nats-server `pb +=; if >mp { pb -=; countSlow }` `server/client.go:2598-2614` for any byte-accumulating buffer (event buffer, log accumulator, API body).
- Provide `LimitByLen` and `LimitBySize` on every internal queue and instantiate with them. Copy nats-server `server/ipqueue.go:75-82` but avoid its default-unbounded call sites (`server/server.go:372`).
- Centralize fitting in one `AllocsFit`-equivalent function that sums `AllocatedResources.Comparable()` and checks `NodeResources - Reserved` `nomad/structs/funcs.go:142-198`. Reuse for workspace and process scopes.
- Own scheduler state in 2-3 goroutines via bounded channels (`server/sched.go:60-65`) with `select { default: fast-fail }`. Add `singleflight` (`server/model_inference_cache.go:66`) and single `activeLoading` lock (`server/sched.go:69`) for spawns.
- Cap streams at parse time: `scanner.Buffer(buf, 8<<20)` (`llm/server.go:160`) and `boundedNumPredict` caps (`llm/llama_server.go:106`). Apply to any LLM token/image stream.
- Thread `ctx.Done()` to `refCount` decrement (`server/sched.go:487-491`) and always pair `acquire <-ch` with `defer release` even on context cancel.
- Expose saturation as gauges, not vanity counters: `slowConsumers`, `stalls`, `waitNanos/maxWaitNanos` (`server/server.go:29-32,412`), `ipQueuesz` (`server/monitor.go:1202`), `vram_free_bytes`, `depth`. Alert on `free < headroom`, not on totals.

**Patterns to delay until needed:**

- Do not build a global OOM circuit breaker before you have per-run byte caps and a bounded scheduler. It is easy to get threshold hysteresis wrong without ledger visibility.
- Do not add weighted fair queuing until you have observed priority starvation (nomad `Priority=100` churn `nomad/eval_broker.go:484`). Fairness without quotas is guesswork.
- Do not parallelize loads per-device until single-load p99 is measured under burst — serialization is correct for launch.

**Decision rules for Aren sizing:**

- If `p95 queue time > 2× queue deadline`, halve `MaxQueue` or add `MaxRunners` — do not grow the channel to hide latency (ollama 512 is a ceiling, not target).
- If `MaxPending` regressions show thundering herd, replace disconnect with `429 + Retry-After` plus queue deadline. Keep micro-stall only for sub-10 ms fan-in.
- If `reserved` diverges from `used` by >10% between `checkAndSyncUsage` scans, shorten the tick from 1500 ms and enforce `wouldExceedLimits` on `used` as well as `reserved`.

**Caution signs:**

- `len(ipQueue)/size(ipQueue)` or `BrokerStats.TotalWaiting` climbs without bound while `enqueuedTime` truncates at 10k (`nomad/eval_broker.go:337`) — truncation hides the metric that would alert.
- Cache map length (`inferenceModelCache`, `modelListCache`, `modelShowCache`) grows linearly with unique keys without TTL — same leak observed in `helper/users/cache.go:15` TTL drift.
- `dios.waitNanos/maxWaitNanos` (`server/server.go:29-32`) and scheduler `stalls` grow while throughput flatlines — semaphore saturated, pipeline depth too low or too high.
- No saturation test that pins hardware (GPU model, driver, RAM, disk), workload, repetitions, and retained heap exists — then any "bounded" claim is anecdotal.

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **Unbounded internal queues with shed defined elsewhere.** nats-server `jsAPIRoutedReqs`/`sendq`/`recvq` created via `newIPQueue(s,name)` without limits `server/server.go:372, server/sendq.go:28`; nomad `EvalBroker` ready/pending/unack unbounded; ollama caches unbounded `server/model_inference_cache.go:19`. Symptom: `push` never returns `errIPQLenLimitReached:85` in production.
- **Static capacity computed once at startup.** nats-server `dynJetStreamConfig` 75% (`server/jetstream.go:2764-2808`) and `diskAvailable` scaling only at `init`; ollama `TotalMemory - allocations` fallback (`llm/llama_server.go:2619`) stale across external consumers. - **Metric-map truncation as bounds.** nomad `enqueuedTime <10k` (`nomad/eval_broker.go:337`) drops tail samples. Symptom: dashboards show stable p50 while heap grows — tail bias.
- **Sub-string OOM classification.** ollama `outOfMemorySubstrings` includes generic `allocation failed` (`llm/status.go:85-96`). Symptom: host RAM failure triggers device eviction then still fails, consuming the one retry.

Symptom: leaked VRAM/disk slot after panic.

## Notable Absences

- **No process-wide heap pressure → load shed in any source.** No periodic `runtime.MemStats`/`GOMEMLIMIT`/`cgroup memory.pressure` polling wired to admission. nats-server checks `GOMEMLIMIT` at startup (`server/jetstream.go:2797`), nomad none (`nomad/eval_broker.go:53` search returned only disk collectors), ollama none before 80% predict gate. For Aren, this is the critical gap to close before Phase 13.
- **No per-workspace or default per-tenant quota.** Quotas exist only as opt-in (nomad `quota` `nomad/blocked_evals.go:371`, nats-server JWT). One run/job without quota can reserve all allocatable memory or all VRAM slots (nomad `count` unlimited, ollama `MaxRunners=0` unlimited `server/sched.go:226`).
- **No queue deadline or fair-queue policy by default.** None of the three enforces `queueDeadline 500ms` with 503/fast-fail, nor weighted-fair dequeuing across keys. nats-server notes `$JS.API.LimitReached` advisory (`server/server.go:377`) but does not enforce; ollama future note is speculative.
- **No bounded disk/history retention process-wide.** nats-server probes disk once (`server/jetstream.go:2787`), nomad GC covers only `AllocDirStats` (`client/gc.go:134`), ollama never quotas `blobs/` or `inference_request_log.go:21-106`. A single large pull/blow-up can fill disk and block all tenants (ollama `ENOSPC` vs `ENOMEM` confusion `server/sched.go:649`).
- **No actionable metrics with limits wired to admission.** nats-server exposes `MaxConn/MaxSubs`/`SlowConsumers` (`server/monitor.go:1235`) but not as alert thresholds; nomad emits counters without thresholds; ollama emits `slog` only (`server/sched.go:613`). No `pending_queue_depth{limit}`, `vram_free{headroom}`, `depth > 0.8*limit → shed` in any repo.
- **No saturation/soak harness with retained-memory assertions.** Throughput micro-benchmarks exist (`server/core_benchmarks_test.go:32`, `scheduler/benchmarks/benchmarks_test.go:74`, `cmd/bench/bench.go:519`) but no 1/4/16 client burst vs steady matrix, no 24-168h soak, no hardware-pinned repetitions asserting `heap < X`, `queue depth bounded`, `goroutines bounded`. This leaves the "bounded" claim unproven.
- **No per-request/per-run cost accounting.** No `tokens*VRAM-seconds` or `eval CPU seconds` ledger per namespace/job/run. nomad measures latency (`nomad/plan_apply.go:526`) not cost; ollama carries `Metrics` in response but does not sum for quota; nats-server counts `slowConsumers` but not per-account cost — all noted as gaps in per-source analyses.
