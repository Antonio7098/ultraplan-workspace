## Executive Summary

This dimension studies the machinery that makes long-running work survive daemon loss, retries without duplicating billable effects, and definitions evolve without breaking inflight histories. The two sources sit at opposite ends of the spectrum and deliberately so.

**temporal-sdk-go (8/10)** is the reference durable-workflow engine: deterministic workflow coroutines replayed from server-persisted history (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:186`), 15 command state machines (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:151`), marker-recorded `SideEffect`/`GetVersion` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1054`, `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:958`), and exhaustive replay fixtures (`test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120`). It proves that replayable control flow plus at-least-once activities can deliver crash resumption, but at ~5k LOC of handlers plus sticky cache, deadlock detector, update/Nexus protocols, and mandatory version gating.

**nomad (3/10)** is the intentional anti-workflow: a workload orchestrator with no workflow/activity/history/replay abstraction. It answers the same requirements with Raft-replicated deterministic FSM (`nomad/fsm.go:250`, `contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13`), bounded two-level retry (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6537`, `nomad/structs/structs.go:6606`), narrow dispatch idempotency (`nomad/structs/structs.go:4476`), and heap-driven periodic timers (`nomad/periodic.go:23`). Its score is not a quality failure but a scope mismatch — it shows what you get when you *defer* workflow machinery and how far Raft + trackers + heap will carry you.

**Convergence despite divergence:** both sources enforce a strict deterministic boundary (pass-the-clock into the replicated log vs deterministic workflow wrappers), treat every external effect as at-least-once and push idempotency to the caller, rate-limit retries with an interval window, deduplicate submissions via caller-supplied keys checked at two layers, and handle worker loss by redelivering whole units of work. Both lack a generic exactly-once effect store and a first-class poison DLQ.

**Divergence that matters for Aren:** Temporal pays history size, version-marker discipline, and operational runbooks to support user-authored, long-running, resumable agent loops; Nomad pays heap-timer staleness and O(children) scans to stay simple and linearizable at 5k nodes. For Aren Phase 16 — explicitly roadmap-deferred until persistence and composition are proven — the synthesis is: borrow Nomad's three cheap primitives now, defer Temporal's replay engine until you have paid evidence of multi-hour LLM sessions that must cold-resume mid-tool-call without re-billing.

## Core Thesis

**Durable workflow machinery is earned complexity: separate deterministic decisions from billable effects at a crisp boundary, persist decisions + dedup keys, and replay only the decisions. Everything else — history iterators, child ownership, durable sleep, versioned workers — is overhead you add lane by lane when a concrete Aren run cannot be recovered cheaper.**

The sources reveal two archetypes that make this thesis falsifiable:

- **Archetype A — Replicated scheduler without user-code replay (nomad).** Durability = Raft log + MemDB snapshots (`nomad/fsm.go:28`, `nomad/fsm.go:147`). The invariant is `FSM.Apply` determinism: callers pass `time.Now()`/`uuid` from the RPC handler (`nomad/job_endpoint.go:2068`) into the mutator; the store never mints them (`contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13`, `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23`). Scheduling decisions are not journaled step-by-step; workers dequeue evals from `EvalBroker` (`nomad/eval_broker.go:19`) / `BlockedEvals` (`nomad/blocked_evals.go:31`), run the scheduler, then commit via `raftApply`. Failure handling is coarse: local `RestartPolicy` (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:26`) + global `RescheduleTracker.Events` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10637`) with `DelayFunction` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6603`). Idempotency is token-at-submission (`nomad/job_endpoint.go:2047` pre-Raft + `nomad/fsm.go:683` post-Raft). Timers are heap-computed (`nomad/periodic.go:469`) not durably scheduled. The lesson: you can operate a large system for years without replay, provided runs are short-lived or re-running from scratch is acceptable.

- **Archetype B — Event-sourced history with deterministic replay (temporal-sdk-go).** Durability = append-only `HistoryEvent` stream; workflow state is never persisted locally beyond sticky cache (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:603`). The invariant is workflow determinism: `syncWorkflowDefinition` dispatcher (`internal/internal_workflow.go:99`) forces `workflow.Channel/Selector/Go` (`workflow/deterministic_wrappers.go:9`), `workflow.Now` (`workflow/deterministic_wrappers.go:174`), `GetVersion`/`SideEffect` markers (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:958`, `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1054`). Effects emit commands driven by per-type state machines (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:183` `CREATED->SENT->INITIATED->COMPLETED`), matched on replay by `matchReplayWithHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1581`). Worker loss evicts cache and calls `resetHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:603`, `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:828`); decisions are at-most-once, activities at-least-once with `RetryPolicy` (`internal/activity.go:151`) and heartbeat throttling (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2186`). The lesson: you pay strict determinism, version markers, and history-size management (`ContinueAsNew` `internal/error.go:192`) to get mid-chain resume and causal debug, but only when tool calls are non-idempotent and sessions outlive the daemon.

Aren is Nomad-shaped today (single-node CLI daemon with synchronous jobs) and should remain so until metric proves Temporal-shaped pain: daemon restart mid-agent loop that must not re-execute a costly tool, or prompt evolution that must not break inflight histories.

## Approach Models

**Model A — Nomad: durability without workflows (the deferred-workflow baseline).**

State is linearizable via HashiCorp Raft; `nomad/fsm.go:250` `Apply(log *raft.Log)` decodes `MessageType` (`nomad/fsm.go:250-289`) and calls typed `apply*` methods; snapshots carry `SnapshotType` tags (`nomad/fsm.go:28-87`) and restore via `StateRestore` (`nomad/state/state_store.go:28`). The architecture doc `contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13-31` bans `time.Now()`/`uuid` inside `StateStore` — enforced culturally and by `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23`. Dispatch mints `DispatchedID(parent, template, time.Now())` at `nomad/job_endpoint.go:2068` before `raftApply`; the FSM re-persists without minting time. Scheduler workers (`nomad/worker.go:88-125`) snapshot at `SnapshotIndex` (`nomad/worker.go:121`) and block via `snapshotMinIndex` (`nomad/worker.go:409`) until Raft catches up, approximating a causal fence. Retry is split: local `RestartPolicy` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6537`, `client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:153`) with 25% jitter (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:250`) for same-node crashes, and global `ReschedulePolicy` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6600`) with `RescheduleTracker` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10637`) storing `RescheduleEvent{RescheduleTime, PrevAllocID, PrevNodeID, Delay}` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10707`) and `RescheduleEligible` leaky-bucket counting only events inside `Interval` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10695`). Timers are `PeriodicDispatch` (`nomad/periodic.go:23`) heap wrapping `container/heap` (`nomad/periodic.go:469`) multiplexing `updateCh`/`launchCh`/`ctx.Done` (`nomad/periodic.go:315`) and deriving child `jobID = parent + PeriodicLaunchSuffix + unix` (`nomad/periodic.go:440`). Duplicate work is suppressed only via dispatch token (`nomad/state/state_store.go:1923`) and `BlockedEvals.duplicates` (`nomad/blocked_evals.go:84`) coalescing queued evals.

Use when runs are short, re-execution is cheap, and scheduling throughput matters more than mid-chain resume.

**Model B — Temporal SDK Go: workflows as replayable coroutines (the earned-complexity reference).**

Every `ScheduleActivityTask`, `StartTimer`, `StartChildWorkflow`, `RecordMarker` becomes a command appended to server-persisted history. Worker `history` struct (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:186`) reorders events via `prepareTask` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:242`) and validates with `matchReplayWithHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1581`) using `isCommandMatchEvent` per type (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1642`). Workflow code runs on `dispatcherImpl` coroutines (`internal/internal_workflow.go:152`, `internal/internal_workflow.go:1044`) not goroutines; `DeterministicKeys`/`NewChannel/NewSelector/Await` (`workflow/deterministic_wrappers.go:9`) are mandatory, `WorkflowRandomStream`/`workflow.Now` replace stdlib. Non-determinism escapes only via `SideEffect`/`MutableSideEffect` markers (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1054`, `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1155`) that record once and replay cached, and `GetVersion` version markers (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:958`, `workflow/workflow.go:529`) plus search-attribute `TemporalChangeVersion`. Retries are `RetryPolicy{InitialInterval,BackoffCoefficient,MaximumInterval,MaximumAttempts,NonRetryableErrorTypes}` (`internal/activity.go:151`, `internal/workflow_test.go:84`) with local-activity split for short vs long backoffs (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1351-1426`); timeouts are `ScheduleToClose/StartToClose/ScheduleToStart/HeartbeatTimeout` on `ScheduleActivityTaskCommandAttributes` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:803`); heartbeats are throttled at 80% of `HeartbeatTimeout` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2186`). Child workflows own deterministic ID `currentRunID + GenerateSequenceID` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:571`) and `ParentClosePolicy` (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:730`); Nexus operations have their own scheduled/cancel state machines (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:123`, `internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:1196`). Checkpointing is `ContinueAsNewError` truncating history (`internal/error.go:192`, `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1897`); sticky `WorkflowCache` LRU (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:603`) with `previousStartedEventID` watermark enables incremental history fetch and `resetHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:717`) on eviction. Replay correctness is regression-tested via `worker.NewWorkflowReplayer().ReplayWorkflowHistoryFromJSONFile` over 30+ JSON histories (`test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120`).

Use when agent loops outlive the daemon, tool effects are non-idempotent/billable, and causal debugging of past decisions is required.

Why models diverge: product shape (orchestrating infra jobs at 5k nodes vs orchestrating long-running business workflows), maturity/operation envelope (Raft quorum latency acceptable for allocs, not for high-frequency workflow signals), and library-vs-application constraints (scheduler defers user-authored determinism problem entirely vs SDK must sandbox it).

## Pattern Catalog

### P1. Pass-the-clock deterministic boundary

- **Problem:** Replicated state or replayed decisions diverge if any replica minting time/UUID/random/map-iteration inside the deterministic layer.
- **Demonstrated by:** Nomad `contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13` + `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23` plus `nomad/job_endpoint.go:2068` vs `nomad/fsm.go:683` split; Temporal `internal/internal_workflow.go:99`, `workflow/deterministic_wrappers.go:9`, `internal/internal_workflow.go:760` `assertNotInReadOnlyState`, `internal/workflow_deadlock.go:12` detector.
- **Why it works:** Moves entropy generation to the caller/RPC handler before replication; deterministic layer becomes pure function of its inputs (Raft log entry or history events). Mechanical check (semgrep / deadlock ticker) catches drift before divergence.
- **When to copy:** Always for any replicated or replayed state in Aren, even before workflows — inject `now time.Time, id string` into the transaction/mutation function; never call `time.Now()`/`uuid` inside it.
- **When overkill/risky:** For purely local in-memory caches with no replication or replay, the overhead of threading clocks is noise.
- **Evidence:** `contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13-31`, `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23`, `nomad/fsm.go:250-289`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:99-101`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow_deadlock.go:12-83`.

### P2. Marker-recorded escape hatch for unavoidable non-determinism

- **Problem:** Workflows must sometimes call `uuid`, read config, or branch on dynamic data without breaking replay of open histories.
- **Demonstrated by:** Temporal `SideEffect`/`MutableSideEffect` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1054-1091`, `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1155-1246`) and `GetVersion` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:958-1002`, `workflow/workflow.go:529-597`) sharing `markerCommandStateMachine` (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:232`) `RecordMarker` path; Nomad's nearest analog is persisting `PeriodicLaunch` timestamp (`nomad/fsm.go:727-733`) as the only marker.
- **Why it works:** First execution records a `MarkerRecorded` event; replay returns cached payload without re-executing the non-deterministic function. Version marker carries `change-id`/`version` and search attribute; `MutableSideEffect` deduplicates via `id+callCounter` and `equals` check.
- **When to copy:** Adopt `SideEffect` for `uuid/rand` inside Aren agent decisions and `GetVersion` for prompt/tool-chain evolution as soon as any history persists beyond a single daemon run. Cost is a marker table; skip if runs are always fresh.
- **When overkill:** For short synchronous jobs that never replay, a marker is pure indirection.
- **Evidence:** `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1054-1091`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/workflow/workflow.go:529-597`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:232-244`.

### P3. Interval-window rate limiting with validated retry policy

- **Problem:** Naive `attempts` counters run forever or reject unsatisfiable configs (e.g., 5 attempts in 1m with 30s delay).
- **Demonstrated by:** Nomad `RescheduleTracker.rescheduleInfo` leaky-bucket counting only events within `failTime - Interval` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10695-10701`) plus `viableAttempts` validator (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6733-6797`) that rejects `Attempts`/`Interval`/`Delay`/`DelayFunction`/`MaxDelay` combos at submit; `RescheduleEligible` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10666`) and jittered client `RestartTracker` (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:250`); Temporal `RetryPolicy` with `NonRetryableErrorTypes` and `MaximumAttempts=1` to disable (`internal/activity.go:151`, `internal/error.go:1088-1119`) and `getRetryBackoff` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1381`) splitting local vs server backoff.
- **Why it works:** Interval window bounds blast radius; validation fails fast at job submit rather than silently never-rescheduling; local vs global knobs mirror restart-vs-reschedule semantics.
- **When to copy:** For any Aren per-run or per-tool retry — store `[]Attempt{Time, PrevRunID, Delay, Error}` like `RescheduleEvent` and enforce `Interval`/`Attempts`/`constant Delay` starting simple. Add exponential/fibonacci only after constant proves insufficient.
- **Risky:** Two knobs doing similar work (`RestartPolicy` vs `ReschedulePolicy`) can mask failures; Temporal's unlimited default (`MaximumAttempts=0`) surprises.
- **Evidence:** `nomad/structs/structs.go:10637-10735`, `nomad/structs/structs.go:6600-6809`, `nomad/structs/alloc.go:439-534`, `client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:26-259`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/activity.go:151-161`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1351-1426`.

### P4. Dual-guard idempotency token at the submission edge

- **Problem:** Clients retry after 5xx/network blip; without a dedup key they create duplicate runs, duplicate billable effects, duplicate cron children.
- **Demonstrated by:** Nomad `WriteRequest.IdempotencyToken` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:449`) stored on child `Job.DispatchIdempotencyToken` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:4476`), checked pre-Raft `snap.CheckIdempotencyToken(ns,parentID,token)` (`nomad/job_endpoint.go:2047-2064`) and again inside FSM `applyUpsertJob` (`nomad/fsm.go:683-694`) to close TOCTOU (fix `.changelog/27353.txt:2`), via prefix scan `JobsByIDPrefix(parentID)` (`nomad/state/state_store.go:1923-1944`) plus HTTP `?idempotency_token` and CLI flag plumbing (`command/agent/http.go:996`, `command/job_dispatch.go:37`); Temporal delegates to server `RequestId` dedup (`internal/client.go:467`) and `WorkflowIdReusePolicy` (`internal/workflow.go:489`) with deterministic `ActivityID`/`WorkflowID` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:798`, `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:571`) and duplicate guards `childWorkflowExistsWithId` (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:1399`), `SignalChannel` buffer check (`internal/workflow.go:928`).
- **Why it works:** Pre-replication check gives fast idempotent response without quorum cost; post-replication guard covers concurrent in-flight entries. Returning existing entity indices makes retries transparent.
- **When to copy:** For Aren's external trigger → run creation edge (webhook, CLI, event bridge) immediately — scoped by `(namespace, parent)` not globally, with explicit retention/GC policy. For activities, use deterministic `ActivityID = "run:<id>:step:<seq>"` and store key in activity implementation before side effect.
- **When overkill:** Global token index is cost; scope narrowly and document bounded idempotency window (GC forgets tokens).
- **Evidence:** `nomad/structs/structs.go:449,4476`, `nomad/job_endpoint.go:2047-2064`, `nomad/fsm.go:683-694`, `nomad/state/state_store.go:1923-1944`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/client.go:467`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:1399-1414`.

### P5. At-least-once effects with at-most-once decisions + deduplication at the effect boundary

- **Problem:** Worker/daemon loss mid-effect leaves ambiguity: did the external call (LLM API, payment, file write) commit?
- **Demonstrated by:** Temporal explicitly separates workflow decisions (at-most-once via history replay `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1287` + `resetHistory` `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:828`) from activities/local-activities/timers/child workflows/Nexus (at-least-once, retried via policy, heartbeat timeout `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2408`, local retry tunneling `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1351`); Nomad demonstrates the scheduler variant — `EvalBroker`/`BlockedEvals` dedup queued evals (`nomad/blocked_evals.go:84`) but workers can race `Dequeue` (`nomad/worker.go:44` 500ms) and Nack on `raftSyncLimit` (`nomad/worker.go:48`) re-running whole scheduling pass, client alloc updates retried at-least-once.
- **Why it works:** Makes the contract honest: callers make effects idempotent rather than assuming exactly-once. Deterministic IDs + retry policy make duplicates detectable.
- **When to copy:** For Aren, treat planner decisions as replayable and LLM/tool calls as activities; require idempotency keys on non-idempotent tools or set `MaximumAttempts=1` and let the workflow decide retry.
- **Risky:** Assuming activity exactly-once because server retries are transparent — Temporal docs warn SideEffect may execute twice if WFT fails before marker commit (`workflow/doc.go:377`).
- **Evidence:** `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_task_handlers.go:828-862`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/activity.go:156`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2186-2400`, `nomad/blocked_evals.go:84-339`, `nomad/worker.go:41-50`.

### P6. Durable timer as history event vs heap-computed cron (two timer strategies)

- **Problem:** Agent needs `sleep`/`poll`/`cron` that survives daemon crash without double-firing or skipping.
- **Demonstrated by:** Temporal `NewTimer`/`RequestCancelTimer` via `timerCommandStateMachine` (`internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:652`, `internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:931`) with `TIMER_STARTED/FIRED/CANCELED` events (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1379`) + short local backoff as ephemeral timer; Nomad `PeriodicDispatch` cron heap (`nomad/periodic.go:23`, `nomad/periodic.go:469-608`) computing `Next` via `job.Periodic.Next(time.Now())` (`nomad/periodic.go:217`) and `ProhibitOverlap` scanning `RunningChildren` (`nomad/periodic.go:87-135`, `nomad/periodic.go:352-364`).
- **Why it works (Temporal):** Timer is a persisted schedule entry + state machine + cancellation linkage; determinism comes from history ordinal, not wall-clock re-derivation.
- **Why it limps (Nomad):** Heap not snapshotted beyond `PeriodicLaunch` last-time (`nomad/fsm.go:727`); failover recomputes `Next` from restored launches (`nomad/leader.go:841`) — simple but susceptible to clock skew/double-launch in same second (`nomad/periodic.go:440`) and O(children) scan per tick.
- **When to copy:** For Aren, use Nomad-style single leader `PeriodicDispatch.run` (`nomad/periodic.go:315`) only for user-visible cron triggers (daily brief) that enqueue an idempotent run; use Temporal-style durable `Sleep` only when the workflow itself must pause mid-chain (human approval) and resume without losing prior tool outputs.
- **Evidence:** `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:652-676`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1379-1388`, `nomad/periodic.go:23-442`, `nomad/leader.go:841-846`.

### P7. Replay validation + fixture harness as version-safety net

- **Problem:** Workflow/cron definition changes silently break inflight histories; detection must be automated.
- **Demonstrated by:** Temporal `matchReplayWithHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1581`, `isCommandMatchEvent` `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1642`) + `WorkflowReplayer.ReplayWorkflowHistoryFromJSONFile` over 30+ histories (`test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120-583`), `deadlockDetector` (`internal/workflow_deadlock.go:12`) and `IsReplaying`/`IsReadOnly` guards (`workflow/workflow.go:723`); Nomad equivalent is absent — only job diff (`nomad/structs/diff.go:292`) decides if eval needed and Raft unknown-type tolerance (`nomad/fsm.go:250`, `nomad/structs/structs.go:153`) keeps log compatible, but no workflow replay.
- **Why it works:** Replay checker turns history incompatibility into a test failure with `[TMPRL1100]` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1616`); fixtures make past histories first-class CI artifacts.
- **When to copy:** For Aren, once any history persists, add `Replayer`-style snapshot tests: freeze a JSONL history per tool-chain version, run new workflow code against it in CI, require `GetVersion`-like branching for any command-order change. Even Nomad-shaped Aren benefits from a single fixture proving `PeriodicLaunch` restore still schedules correctly.
- **Evidence:** `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1581-1632`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120-132`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow_deadlock.go:12-83`, `nomad/fsm.go:250-261`, `nomad/structs/diff.go:292-304`.

## Key Differences

**Durability primitive.** Nomad: Raft log + MemDB + snapshots; `Apply` is pure over bytes (`nomad/fsm.go:250`); scheduler holds only eval indices, not step journals. Temporal: event-sourced `HistoryEvent` protobuf stream; worker `history` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:186`) is source of truth, `WorkflowCache` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:603`) is ephemeral; completeness of history enables mid-chain resume, at cost of history size and `ContinueAsNew` truncation (`internal/error.go:192`).

**Determinism enforcement.** Nomad: cultural rule + single semgrep on store boundary (`.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23`); no sandbox for user workflows because users don't author them. Temporal: language-level sandbox — coroutine dispatcher (`internal/internal_workflow.go:82`), mandatory wrappers (`workflow/deterministic_wrappers.go:9`), `assertNotInReadOnlyState` panics (`internal/internal_workflow.go:760`), 1s deadlock ticker (`internal/workflow_deadlock.go:72`).

**Retry semantics.** Nomad: two knobs at two layers — local `RestartPolicy` immediate jittered restarts (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:26`) plus global `ReschedulePolicy` with interval-window and `DelayFunction`/`MaxDelay` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6600`). Temporal: single `RetryPolicy` per activity/child/Nexus (`internal/activity.go:151`) with server-computed backoff, `NonRetryableErrorTypes`, heartbeat timeout detection (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2360`), and local-activity short-path retries.

## Tradeoffs

**Full history replay vs Raft FSM determinism.**
- *Benefit (Temporal):* Mid-chain crash resume, causal debugging, and deterministic replay tests; changes to tool/prompt chains are versionable without losing inflight sessions.
- *Cost:* Mandatory determinism hygiene, history growth (meta on every WFT `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1979`, `payloadSizeError` `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2557`), `ContinueAsNewSuggestedReasons` fragmentation (`workflow/workflow.go:66`), deployment versioning coexistence (`internal/internal_worker.go:154`).
- *Best-fit:* Long-running agent sessions with non-idempotent tools or human-in-the-loop pauses. For Aren today (short synchronous jobs) the Nomad alternative — re-run from last completed tool record — is cheaper.
- *Failure mode:* Forgotten `GetVersion` wraps → `nondeterministic workflow: missing/extra replay command` panic and `WorkflowPanicPolicy BlockWorkflow` infinite retry (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`) requiring operator terminate/fix deploy.
- *Alternative seen:* Nomad avoids the problem entirely by never replaying user code; upgrades tolerate unknown `MessageType`s instead.

**Global retry policy per effect vs split restart/reschedule.**
- *Benefit (Temporal single policy):* One place to configure `MaximumAttempts`/`NonRetryable`/`HeartbeatTimeout`; works uniformly for activities/child/Nexus updates.
- *Cost:* No distinction between same-node fast path and cross-node escape; unlimited default (`MaximumAttempts=0`) surprises; jitter not configured in SDK (server computes).
- *Benefit (Nomad split):* Keeps p99 low via jittered local restarts (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:250`) while still escaping bad nodes via interval-window reschedules; `viableAttempts` validator prevents unsatisfiable configs.
- *Failure mode:* Two knobs mask root cause — local restarts hide failures that should have counted as reschedule attempts; global policy can silently stop rescheduling after `Attempts` exhausted with no poison queue (operator must `ForceReschedule` `nomad/structs/alloc.go:1558`).
- *Best-fit context:* Use split only when you control node identity (infrastructure scheduling); for Aren agent tool retries, start with a single constant-delay policy.

**Narrow parent-scoped token vs global/workflow-scoped dedup.**
- *Benefit (Nomad dispatch token):* Prefix scan `JobsByIDPrefix(parentID)` (`nomad/state/state_store.go:1924`) is O(children), no global index, dual-guard covers TOCTOU, transparent return of existing `CreateIndex`/`ModifyIndex`.
- *Cost:* Token forgotten after GC (`nomad/core_sched.go:887`); cross-parent collisions undetected; not generalized to other writes.
- *Benefit (Temporal layered):* `RequestId` + `WorkflowIdReusePolicy` + deterministic IDs cover workflows/activities/children/signals uniformly.
- *Failure mode:* Same token after GC creates duplicate child; workflow ID collision with default `GenerateSequence` without explicit `ActivityID` yields silent overwrite vs clean dedup hit.
- *Best-fit:* Narrow scope is correctly cheap if GC window > client retry window; global if effect is billable long after GC.

**Heap cron vs durable timer history.**
- *Benefit (Nomad heap):* Tiny snapshots (only `PeriodicLaunch` last-time `nomad/fsm.go:727`); O(1) next-tick compute; no history growth.
- *Cost:* `RunningChildren` scan is O(children×evals×allocs) per tick (`nomad/periodic.go:87-133`); double-launch within same second collides on `DerivedID` second-granularity (`nomad/periodic.go:440`); missed tick after failover recomputed not at-most-once.
- *Benefit (Temporal timer):* Timer is a persisted `TIMER_STARTED/FIRED/CANCELED` event with cancel state machine; survives eviction and is replay-exact.
- *Failure mode:* Storing a timer per firing without cancellation hygiene leaks history; heap without persisted handle loses durable sleep semantics.
- *Best-fit:* Heap for cron-as-trigger that enqueues an idempotent run; durable timer only for in-workflow `Sleep` that must survive.

**Eager/local retries vs server-timer retries.**
- *Benefit (Temporal local-activity tunnel `internal/internal_worker.go:393`):* Short backoffs complete in-process without holding WFT open, via `time.AfterFunc` + `laRetryCh`; long backoffs create server timer marker (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1351`) so workflow can suspend.
- *Cost:* Separate `localActivityCounterID`, `pendingLaTasks` rebuild, not heartbeatable, not routable, stalls on worker shutdown `laTunnel.stopCh` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:984`).
- *Alternative (Nomad):* Local restarts block only the alloc on that client; server doesn't know — simpler but non-durable across client death.

## Decision Guide

Answer four questions; the branches map directly to the two archetypes.

**1. Must a run survive daemon loss mid-tool-call without re-billing or losing prior tool outputs?**
- No — runs are short or tools are idempotent/cheap (current Aren) → stay Nomad-shaped: record events in a simple journal, re-run from last completed tool on restart, clone `RescheduleTracker` for retry delays.
- Yes — e.g., multi-hour research workflow calling paid APIs, human approval pause, must resume at exact step → adopt Temporal-shaped minimal subset: event log (sqlite/JSONL `HistoryEvent` equivalent), deterministic decision replay, activities with `RetryPolicy{MaximumAttempts:1}` for non-idempotent tools, heartbeat for long LLM calls, `SideEffect` for UUID.

**2. How severe is the duplicate-effect cost?**
- Cheap duplicate (re-fetch, re-render) → client-side retry with bounded interval and narrow idempotency token at run-submit only (Nomad dispatch pattern `nomad/job_endpoint.go:2047` + `nomad/fsm.go:683`).
- Expensive duplicate (payment, external mutation, LLM token burn) → require deterministic `ActivityID = "run:<id>:step:<seq>"` (Temporal `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:798` pattern), store idempotency key *before* external call in activity implementation, and make activities check the store first.

**3. Must workflow definition changes stay compatible with inflight histories?**
- Infrequent or jobs are short-lived → Nomad-style: canonicalize (`nomad/fsm.go:681`) and tolerate unknown `MessageType` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:153`); in-flight allocs keep embedded task group copy (`nomad/structs/alloc.go:104`).
- Frequent prompt/tool-chain iteration with hours-long inflight runs → Temporal-style: `GetVersion(changeID, DefaultVersion, 1)` gates (`workflow/workflow.go:529`), `MutableSideEffect` for config (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:1155`), plus `WorkflowReplayer` fixtures (`test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120`) in CI — missing the gate is a `nondeterministic` panic.

**4. What is your operation envelope?**
- Need linearizable scheduling at quorum latency, 5k nodes, Raft operability — borrow Nomad's `EvalBroker`/`BlockedEvals` dedup and `snapshotMinIndex` fence, not sticky cache/history iterators.
- Need causal debugging and `ContinueAsNew` history truncation for unbounded agent loops — accept Temporal's history size metering (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1979`, `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2557`), `WorkflowPanicPolicy` runbook (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`), and payload visitor complexity.

**Minimum viable for Aren Phase 16 (from both per-source reports):**
1. Event log + deterministic replay of tool-use decisions vs activity wrappers (Temporal `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:803` shape, but embedded not Temporal server).
2. `SideEffect` for UUID/random + `GetVersion` for prompt evolution (Temporal) or pass-the-clock into mutation (Nomad) — pick one and enforce in CI.
3. Interval-window retry + dual-guard idempotency token at run-submit (Nomad `nomad/structs/structs.go:10637`, `nomad/state/state_store.go:1923`) + deterministic activity IDs for effect dedup (Temporal).
Defer: child workflows, Nexus/remote operations, durable timers beyond cron-trigger, sessions, update protocol, worker deployment versioning, deadlock detector, eager activities.

## Practical Tips

1. **Enforce pass-the-clock now, before any workflow.** Mirror Nomad `contributing/architecture-state-store.md:13` verbatim for any replicated Aren state: thread `now time.Time, id string` into your `Apply`/`UpsertRun` from the RPC/CLI handler; add a semgrep rule like `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23` banning `time.Now()`/`uuid.New()` inside `state_store.go` and `fsm.go`. Cost ~0, prevents whole class of Raft divergence or replay skew.

2. **Add an idempotency token on run submit as your cheapest exactly-once-ish guard.** Lift Nomad's shape: `WriteRequest.IdempotencyToken` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:449`) → `Run.DispatchIdempotencyKey` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:4476`) → `?idempotency_token` on `POST /runs` (`command/agent/http.go:996`) and `--idempotency-token` on CLI (`command/job_dispatch.go:37`); check prefix-scoped `CheckIdempotencyToken` (`nomad/state/state_store.go:1923`) both before quorum and inside commit. Scope by `(workspace, parent)` and document bounded window (GC `nomad/core_sched.go:887` forgets tokens). Do this before history — clients that retry after 5xx already need it.

3. **Start retries with a persisted tracker, not an in-memory counter.** Define `RunRetryEvent{AttemptTime, PrevAttemptID, ToolName, Delay, Error}` like Nomad `RescheduleEvent` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10707`), enforce `Attempts`/`Interval`/`constant Delay` via `RescheduleEligible` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10666`) and validate at run-template submit via `viableAttempts` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:6733`). Surface `LastReschedule` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10640`) as `RunRetryStatus`. Constant delay avoids over-engineering exponential/fibonacci until needed.

4. **Treat worker loss as redelivery, not exactly-once.** Both sources agree: workers `Dequeue` with timeout (`nomad/worker.go:44` 500ms) and Nack on sync lag (`nomad/worker.go:48` `raftSyncLimit:5s`); Temporal evicts sticky cache and `resetHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:828-862`). Design activities to tolerate a second execution: deterministic `ActivityID` (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:798`) keyed by run+sequence, idempotent check outside replay, and heartbeat for long LLM calls (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2186` throttled at 80% `HeartbeatTimeout`).

5. **Freeze replay fixtures before you change a workflow.** Even if you stay Nomad-shaped, freeze one JSONL history per workflow version; when you adopt any replay, copy Temporal `test/replaytests/replay_test.go:120` `ReplayWorkflowHistoryFromJSONFile` pattern into Aren CI. Failures should cite `[TMPRL1100]`-style mismatches (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1616`) not Silent data divergence.

6. **Use `ContinueAsNew` as intentional checkpoint, not magic GC.** Temporal's `ContinueAsNewError` (`internal/error.go:192`, `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1897`) truncates history when `HistorySizeTooLarge` (`workflow/workflow.go:73`). For Aren, do not auto-truncate — caller returns `ContinueAsNew` explicitly when token/history budget exceeded; without it, large payloads hit `payloadSizeError` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2557`) or `changeVersionSearchAttrSizeLimit` overflow (`internal/internal_event_handlers.go:32`).

7. **Defer child workflows, Nexus, sessions, updates, and deployment versioning** until you demonstrate pain from manual checkpointing (e.g., fan-out where `ParentClosePolicy` `internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:730` or durable `Update` `internal/internal_event_handlers.go:174` actually needed). None are justified for synchronous single-node Aren.

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **Determinism leak into replicated/replayable code.** Symptom: `time.Now()`/`rand`/`map range` inside `FSM.Apply` or `workflow.Context` without `GetVersion`/`SideEffect` gate. Detector: CI semgrep (Nomad `.semgrep/fsm_time.yml:23`) and replay checker `matchReplayWithHistory` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1581`) or deadlock detector (`internal/workflow_deadlock.go:12`) panic. Failure: Raft divergence or `[TMPRL1100] nondeterministic workflow` blocking all open runs until deploy fix — see Temporal `WorkflowPanicPolicy BlockWorkflow` infinite `WorkflowTaskTimeout` retry (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`).

- **Assuming exactly-once effects.** Symptom: activity/tool writes (file, git commit, payment) without idempotency key. Both sources show at-least-once (`nomad/blocked_evals.go:84` dedup only queued evals; `internal/activity.go:151` `MaximumAttempts=0` unlimited default). Caution when reviewers claim "retry is safe" — demand the `ActivityID` dedup store proof.

- **Idempotency scope illusion.** Symptom: global `IdempotencyToken` expected to dedup forever. Nomad's token is prefix-scoped (`nomad/state/state_store.go:1924`), stored on child, forgotten after GC (`nomad/core_sched.go:887`); second dispatch after GC creates a duplicate with no error — caller cannot distinguish via status code. Fix: bound window + retention table or return `DispatchedJobID` with a `deduped: true` flag.

- **Clock-skewed retry/timer arithmetic.** Symptom: using wall-clock `failTime` from different servers for interval window (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10697` `UTC().UnixNano() - lastAttempt` vs `interval.Nanoseconds()`; `nomad/structs/alloc.go:526` `failTime.Add(nextDelay)`) or cron `Next` (`nomad/periodic.go:217` `time.Now().In(...)`). Skew makes an alloc flip inside/outside window inconsistently; periodic `DerivedID` (`nomad/periodic.go:440` second-granularity) collides on same-second double-launch.

- **Heap timer treated as durable signal.** Symptom: persisting only `PeriodicLaunch` timestamp (`nomad/fsm.go:727`) while testing as if each tick were a durably scheduled work item with cancellation. Missed tick after leader failover is recomputed (`nomad/leader.go:841`) not preserved; `ProhibitOverlap` re-scans children/evals/allocs (`nomad/periodic.go:87-133`) at O(n) per tick — alert when cron throughput ties to quorum latency (`nomad/periodic.go:75` `raftApply` per firing).

- **Poison work silently starving.** Symptom: client `IsRecoverable` gate (`client/allocrunner/taskrunner/restarts/restarts.go:210`) exists but server `RescheduleEligible` has no poisoning; bad image consumes all `Attempts` then transitions to dead with no DLQ (`nomad/structs/structs.go:10666`); Temporal activity panics (`internal/error.go:836`) retry until `MaximumAttempts` then fail; workflow panics block forever (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`). Operate `ForceReschedule` (`nomad/structs/alloc.go:1558`) or `Terminate` runbook from day one; absence of listing/clearing poison at scale is flagged open in Nomad gaps.

- **`BlockedEvals` Nack storm / heartbeat loss storm.** Symptom: `EvalReapCancelableInterval` pruning (`nomad/config.go:271`) masking scheduling starvation; heartbeat throttling (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2186`) losing last details on graceful shutdown (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2422` only flushes if not completed). Measure queue depth + heartbeat timeout rate as early brittleness signals.

- **Unversioned workflow evolution.** Symptom: adding/removing/reordering `ExecuteActivity`/`NewTimer`/`ExecuteChildWorkflow` calls without `GetVersion` marker. Replay fixtures absent. Detection: adder of new `workflow` branch does not add a replay JSON file. Failure: every open history panics with `lookup failed for scheduledEventID` (`test/replaytests/replay_test.go:152`) after deploy.

## Notable Absences

- **Exactly-once storage-level dedup for activity side effects** in both sources. Nomad has no generic write dedup beyond parent-scoped dispatch; SDK grep shows only `RequestId` at client level (`internal/client.go:467`, `internal/internal_workflow_client.go:1351`) and workflow reuse policies (`internal/workflow.go:489`), no SDK dedup table for activity payloads — server source needed to verify transactional completion.

- **Generic exactly-once effect boundary in either source.** Both push idempotency to the effect implementer (deterministic activity IDs + caller-supplied token). There is no shared transactional outbox that guarantees an activity's side effect commits exactly once with its history event.

- **Operator poison-pill remediation UX / DLQ.** Nomad: only `ReasonUnrecoverableError` string and `LastRescheduleFailed` flag; no poison-allocation table. Temporal: `WorkflowPanicPolicy FailWorkflow vs BlockWorkflow` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`, `internal/error.go:836`) but no auto-DLQ; requires CLI/API terminate/reset outside SDK — not present in SDK repo.

- **First-class history/checkpoint truncation automation.** Nomad: no workflow history at all; snapshots only. Temporal: exposes `HistorySizeTooLarge` reason (`workflow/workflow.go:73`) but caller must explicitly return `ContinueAsNewError` (`internal/error.go:192`); no auto-policy, leaving open when Aren should trigger truncation.

- **Determinism checker / replay fixtures for Nomad.** Explicitly absent — reported gap: no replay fixtures, no determinism checker, no worker-loss redelivery integration test analogous to Temporal's; `nomad/worker_test.go:409` and `blocked_evals_test.go:753` cover snapshot visibility/backpressure not at-most-once placement.

- **Cancellation scope fan-out for dispatched children.** Nomad sets `ParentID` (`nomad/job_endpoint.go:2069`) but no evidence of propagating `Stop`/`Evict` (`nomad/structs/alloc.go:104`, `nomad/fsm.go:464`) to dispatched children; Temporal has explicit `ParentClosePolicy` but no evidence of SDK-level credential/identity propagation across hops beyond per-alloc `AuthenticatedIdentity` (`nomad/structs/structs.go:502`).

- **Direct worker-loss integration tests covering duplicate-delivery.** Neither per-source report located a named `worker-loss`/`duplicate-delivery` test in the studied repos; Temporal relies on server tests, Nomad on eval-broker tests — coverage assumption unverified within evidence.

## Open Questions

1. **Worker loss and duplicate execution contracts.** No explicit "worker crash mid-plan then redelivered" integration test was found in either source (`nomad/worker*`, `nomad/blocked_evals*`, `test/workflow_test.go`, `testsuite` search boundaries). 3. **Cancellation scope for hierarchical runs.** Nomad `DesiredStatus`/`DesiredTransition` (`nomad/structs/alloc.go:104`, `nomad/structs/alloc.go:1492`) can `Stop`/`Evict` one alloc but no per-workflow fan-out to `ParentID` children was found; Temporal has `ParentClosePolicy` and `RequestCancelTimer/Nexus` but validator must remain side-effect-free. How should Aren propagate `Cancel(run)` to child tool invocations/timers atomically?

4. **GC vs idempotency horizon.** Nomad retains dispatch tokens only until job GC (`nomad/core_sched.go:887`); Temporal's `RequestId` horizon is server-side and not visible in SDK evidence. How long should Aren retain `RunIdempotencyToken → RunID` mappings, and whether to tombstone vs bounded-window documentation, remains undecided.

5. **Poison work operating model.** Neither source provides a first-class DLQ or poison-allocation table — Nomad has only `ReasonUnrecoverableError` and `ForceReschedule` (`nomad/structs/alloc.go:1558`); Temporal relies on `WorkflowPanicPolicy` (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1332`) requiring external terminate/reset. What is Aren's run-failure state listing, alerting, and clearing UX at scale?

6. **When to auto-truncate history.** Both sources leave the trigger to caller — Nomad has no history, Temporal `ContinueAsNewSuggestedReasons` (`workflow/workflow.go:66`) but no auto-truncation (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:1897` requires explicit `ContinueAsNewError`). How will Aren decide `HistorySizeTooLarge` vs token-budget threshold, and how to keep queries working across fragments?

7. **Method expandability and wording that can propagate.** Example #5 from evidence: `ListWorkflows` exists but no pointer on what to implement — separate workflows for volunteer, innovation, performance & sensitive depth-threshold — any single source can be used.

8. **Storage-level deduplication for activity side effects.** Grep for `idempotency/dedup` found only client-side keys; no SDK dedup table. Need server-source confirmation that activity completion is transactional with task dequeue, and whether Aren needs its own outbox before effect.
