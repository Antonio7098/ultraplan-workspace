## Executive Summary

Five Go systems — a concurrency library, an agent application, a model server, a container CLI, and a workflow SDK — answer this dimension's four questions with radically different mechanisms, yet converge on the same three-part decomposition: a *propagation* channel for the cancel request, a *join guarantee* for every spawned worker, and a *terminal publication* signal that fires only after cleanup completes. Quality differences track which of the three parts each source enforces mechanically versus by convention.

The strongest convergent findings: (1) terminal truth lives in a guaranteed channel close or a synchronous join, never in droppable intermediate events — docker-agent emits a best-effort `StreamStoppedEvent` then treats only the deferred channel close as authoritative (`sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/loop.go:187-211`, `327-337`), and temporal converts shutdown from a race into a phase transition by having a dedicated closer goroutine close `taskQueueCh` only after all pollers exit (`sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_worker_base.go:467-478`). (2) Primary-failure preservation is achieved either by first-wins latches (conc's CAS catcher, `sources/conc/panics/panics.go:26-31`; temporal's first-error-under-lock, `sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_worker.go:2330-2353`) or merge-don't-substitute error joining (runc's `%w; %w` init-fatal merging, `sources/runc/libcontainer/container_linux.go:406-421`). (3) Anything supervising external processes needs an escalation ladder — SIGTERM/SIGINT → timed grace → SIGKILL → unconditional Wait — and ollama proves the negative case: its llama-server close path waits unboundedly after SIGKILL, which wedges the scheduler under a lock and makes further Ctrl-C inert (`sources/ollama/llm/llama_server.go:2569-2589`, `sources/ollama/server/sched.go:1695-1711`, `sources/ollama/server/routes.go:2021-2030`).

The most instructive weakness is universal: **shutdown idempotency under concurrent callers is best-effort everywhere**. conc's `Pool.Wait` closes the task channel on every call, so two concurrent callers panic (`sources/conc/pool/pool.go:72-82`); temporal's top-level `AggregatedWorker.Stop()` guards `close(stopC)` with a non-atomic check (`sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_worker.go:1614-1640`); even runc's otherwise-strong state machine re-runs poststop hooks on repeated `Destroy()` (`sources/runc/libcontainer/state_linux.go:64-66,104-106`). Only docker-agent layers enough mechanisms (CAS flags, `sync.Once`, published-stop handshakes) to survive concurrent supervision, and only temporal backs shutdown behavior with deterministic tests. Relatedly, goroutine-leak detection exists in exactly one of five sources (temporal's goleak + `testing/synctest` battery, `sources/temporal-sdk-go/test/integration_test.go:109-134`); ollama has a single `NumGoroutine` regression test (`sources/ollama/progress/progress_test.go:49-73`); runc checks fds but not goroutines (`sources/runc/libcontainer/integration/exec_test.go:1693-1778`); conc and docker-agent have none.

For Aren Phase 1, the transferable core is small and consistent across sources: one owner per concurrency scope with a join guarantee, cause-typed cancellation from day one, record-outcome-before-cancel ordering, guaranteed-close terminal publication, buffered(1) result channels, and escalation ladders around anything with a pid.

One caveat applies throughout: all five analyses report the cited Aren requirement documents were inaccessible under source-isolation rules, so alignment claims rest on the dimension text alone.

## Core Thesis

**Cancellation, ownership, and cleanup are three independent disciplines that fail independently — and the dominant failure seam is always the same place: the moment a cancel request meets cleanup that can itself block, race, or be re-entered.** Every source here implements propagation somehow (contexts, signals, or channel closes); the score spread comes from whether joins are guaranteed, waits are bounded, and shutdown survives a second caller.

Three regularities hold across all five:

- **Ownership strength predicts graceful degradation.** conc answers "who waits?" structurally — one raw `go` statement in the entire library (`sources/conc/waitgroup.go:30`) — so even its weaknesses (thin cancellation, no leak tests) are contained. ollama answers it partially — nothing joins its two scheduler loops (`sources/ollama/server/routes.go:2060-2067`) and streaming producers have no owner at all (`sources/ollama/server/routes.go:649-792`) — so its failures compound: a parked producer holds a semaphore slot that pins the model (`sources/ollama/llm/llama_server.go:1542`, `1708`). When ownership is ambiguous, every later bug lands there.
- **Boundedness separates the top scorers from the pack.** docker-agent bounds nearly every wait (3s shell grace, 30s toolset budget, 500ms pipe delay, 5-min SSE idle timeout — `sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/shell/shell.go:221-232`, `cmd/root/run.go:1381-1387`, `pkg/runtime/streaming.go:19-39`) and temporal engineers bounds at every layer (70s poll cap, `WorkerStopTimeout`, bounded WaitGroup helper — `sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_task_pollers.go:37-39`, `internal/internal_utils.go:161-181`). The 6/10 sources each have at least one unbounded wait on a primary path: ollama's `<-s.done` reap, runc's pipe-drain `wg.Wait()` (`sources/runc/tty.go:177-183`), conc's indefinite `Wait` against a task that ignores its context.
- **Idempotency is chosen, not inherited.** Go primitives give you repeat-safe `cancel()` and `wg.Wait()` for free; everything else — channel closes, hook execution, bookkeeping resets — must be made re-entrant deliberately (state machine, CAS, `sync.Once`, handshake) or protected by an ownership rule that says only one caller may ever drive shutdown.

Aren's Phase 1 contract needs all three legs, and none of them requires importing framework bulk: the load-bearing mechanisms range from ~15 LOC (CAS latch) through ~100 LOC (join point with ordered close) to one supervisor type (adopt/reap/handshake).

## Approach Models

**conc — ownership as a type invariant.** The library's entire answer is one primitive: a panic-catching `WaitGroup` whose `Go` registers the child before spawning and whose `Wait` truthfully means "all spawned goroutines exited" (`sources/conc/waitgroup.go:28-43`). Pools, streams, and iterators all compose it rather than reimplementing tracking, yielding a single auditable spawn site. Cancellation is deliberately not core: `WithContext` converts a configured pool into a `ContextPool` holding one derived context (`sources/conc/pool/pool.go:140-148`), and stopping work relies entirely on tasks selecting on `ctx.Done()`. Shutdown is close-and-drain, enabling struct reuse via an `initOnce` reset (`sources/conc/pool/pool.go:77-79`). This model fits a stdlib-only library whose callers own supervision; it breaks down exactly when the caller doesn't honor the contract (forgotten `Wait`, concurrent `Wait`).

**docker-agent — two lifetimes with an explicit detachment boundary.** Request contexts drive turns (per-message `WithCancel`, `sources/docker-agent/pkg/cli/runner.go:88`, `pkg/tui/page/chat/chat.go:1301`); connection lifetimes are severed at the boundary (`context.WithoutCancel`, `sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/runtime.go:679`; MCP stash-the-parent pattern, `pkg/tools/mcp/cancellable_parent.go:14-59`). Long-lived connections share one Supervisor parameterized by Connector + Policy, with adoption/reaping of timed-out connects (`pkg/tools/lifecycle/supervisor.go:302-402`). Termination is negotiated per substrate: OS processes get a SIGTERM→SIGKILL ladder (`pkg/tools/builtin/shell/shell.go:221-232`), HTTP gets `CancelCauseFunc` closing the TCP body (`pkg/runtime/streaming.go:60-63`), blocked prompts get select-on-ctx (`pkg/runtime/toolexec/dispatcher.go:809-845`). Uniquely among the sources, cancellation is *conversational*: user cancels become explicit tool_result errors fed back to the model (`pkg/runtime/toolexec/dispatcher.go:1111-1131`). This model fits a long-lived interactive application juggling request, session, and connection scopes.

**ollama — arbitration by channel, termination by signal.** A root context wired to SIGTERM fans into a scheduler context and per-request contexts (`sources/ollama/server/routes.go:2002-2032`); a two-goroutine scheduler owns runner state through four maxQueue-sized channels (`server/sched.go:60-81`); each runner wrapper owns an `exec.Cmd` plus a detached reaper goroutine and `done` channel (`llm/llama_server.go:1005-1030`). Detached work (downloads/uploads) survives client disconnect by design, lifetime managed by refcount→`CancelFunc` (`server/download.go:436-444`). Idempotency of unload comes from PID matching plus map-presence under a documented lock-ordering rule (`server/sched.go:429-451`, `1753-1754`). The model is coherent, but the edges — streaming publication and final shutdown — were left unowned, which is precisely where its failures live.

**runc — signals and kernels instead of contexts.** The root context is `context.Background()` and every action discards it (`sources/runc/main.go:184`, `run.go:77`); "cancellation" is Unix signal forwarding to the container init, and completion is the reaped exit status converted to `os.Exit(status)` (`signals.go:93-99`, `run.go:81-87`). Ownership is asymmetric: three IO copiers tracked on a WaitGroup and joined in ordered `tty.Close()` (`tty.go:170-187`), at least five goroutine families intentionally detached and bounded by process lifetime (`tty.go:128,137,145-151`; `events.go:77-86`). Idempotency is the strongest of the five: a mutex-guarded state machine re-derives reality via `refreshState()` and start-time-checked liveness before every destructive step (`libcontainer/state_linux.go:39-67`; `libcontainer/container_linux.go:919-952`). This model works because each invocation exits — process death reclaims what the code never joined — and would be a liability embedded in a long-lived supervisor.

**temporal-sdk-go — staged shutdown as a designed protocol.** One `baseWorker` owns every runtime goroutine on two WaitGroups (`stopWG`, `pollerWG`), stopped by closing one `stopCh` in a fixed sequence: close stopCh → cancel limiter ctx → bounded WaitGroup wait → cancel task limiter → background-cancel with `ErrWorkerShutdown` cause (`internal/internal_worker_base.go:899-926`). Aggregated shutdown is staged top-down with semantic ordering — the workflow worker fully stops before `localActivityStopC` closes so pending local activities finish (`internal/internal_worker.go:389-397,466-472`). Cancellation carries causes (`context.WithCancelCause` at root and per-task; activities distinguish server-cancel vs shutdown vs pause via `context.Cause`, `internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2480-2485`). Result publication is decoupled from lifecycle: completion RPCs run on background-rooted contexts so shutdown never eats a finished result (`internal/internal_task_pollers.go:1520`). This is the most engineered model, and its residual flaws are correspondingly precise: the very top of the staged sequence is the one non-atomic link.

These are responses to product shape, not quality tiers: a library cannot enforce its caller's discipline; a CLI's process exit is a legitimate garbage collector; a server managing GPU-driving subprocesses cannot rely on cooperation; an SDK selling a documented graceful-shutdown promise owes users a grace window and tests proving it.

## Pattern Catalog

### P1. Single join-point owner (one spawn site per scope)

- **Problem:** "who waits for every goroutine started on behalf of a run?" must have a mechanical answer.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc — all spawns route through one panic-catching WaitGroup; exactly one raw `go` statement in the library (`sources/conc/waitgroup.go:28-34`, `30`). temporal — every poller, dispatcher, and slot reservation counted on `baseWorker.stopWG` and awaited under a timeout (`internal/internal_worker_base.go:447-478`, `910-918`).
- **Why it works:** the ownership tree becomes auditable by grep; `Wait` semantics are provable instead of conventional.
- **Copy when:** any scope spawns more than trivially. This is the cheapest high-value pattern in the study.
- **Risky/overkill when:** never really — runc's per-subsystem split shows the degraded version still functions, but only because process exit backstops it (`sources/runc/tty.go:60-62` vs untracked stdin copiers at `56-59`).
- **Evidence:** conc's forgotten-`Wait` leak is silent precisely because nothing tracks outstanding handles (`sources/conc/README.md:55-61` states the rule; `sources/conc/go.mod:5` shows no enforcement dependency).

### P2. Guaranteed-close terminal publication

- **Problem:** consumers need one authoritative "all work and cleanup done" signal that cannot be dropped, reordered, or faked.
- **Demonstrated by:** docker-agent — deferred `finalizeEventChannel` guarantees close on every exit path including early returns and panics; intermediate `StreamStopped` events are explicitly droppable, only the close is authoritative (#3070/#3074 rationale, `sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/loop.go:187-211`, `327-337`). temporal — a closer goroutine waits on `pollerWG` then closes `taskQueueCh`, letting the dispatcher's range loop terminate deterministically (`internal/internal_worker_base.go:467-478`). runc publishes exit status only after `process.Wait()` flushes pipe handling (`sources/runc/signals.go:81-85`).
- **Why it works:** channel close is a happens-before edge Go already guarantees; events are lossy by nature. Making close the truth makes `for range` a correct join everywhere.
- **Copy when:** any producer/consumer lifecycle. Pair with ordered defers so unlock precedes close (docker-agent's `defer Unlock` before `close(streamChan)`, `sources/docker-agent/pkg/server/session_manager.go:1032-1040`).
- **Evidence:** temporal's synctest tests pin that Stop returns after exactly `stopTimeout`, proving the close-ordering machinery composes under virtual time (`internal/internal_worker_base_test.go:1084-1091`).

### P3. Cause-typed cancellation

- **Problem:** receivers must distinguish why cancellation fired (user cancel vs shutdown vs pause vs sibling failure) to respond correctly.
- **Demonstrated by:** temporal — `context.WithCancelCause` at worker root (`internal/internal_worker.go:2289-2295`) and per-task (`internal/internal_task_handlers.go:2389-2399`); discrimination via `IsCanceledError(context.Cause(ctx))` naming `ErrWorkerShutdown` explicitly (`2480-2485`). docker-agent — `batchCtx = WithCancelCause(ctx)` distinguishes user-cancel from hook-stop messages (`pkg/runtime/toolexec/dispatcher.go:240-276`).
- **Why it works:** one context serves many masters without sentinel-channel proliferation, and downstream code branches on typed causes instead of guessing.
- **Copy when:** more than one actor can trigger cancellation — which is true of essentially every system in this study.
- **Watch out:** conc documents the sibling-noise problem: cancel-on-error makes siblings publish `context.Canceled` alongside the causal error unless suppressed (`sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:60-77`). Causes exist partly to fix this class of confusion.

### P4. Record the causal outcome before triggering cancellation

- **Problem:** cancellation side effects (sibling `context.Canceled`, end-of-run defaults) can overwrite or mask the triggering failure.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc records the error into the pool *before* calling `cancel()` so late `context.Canceled` errors can't displace it under `WithFirstError` — flagged in-code as a "leaky abstraction warning" (`sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:39-47`). temporal stores only the first fatal error under a lock and `Run` returns that stored error rather than a shutdown artifact (`internal/internal_worker.go:2330-2353`, `1604-1609`). docker-agent re-checks `ctx.Err()` before publishing endReason so cancellation isn't misreported as success (`pkg/runtime/loop.go:750-764`). runc merges init fatals into the primary error rather than substituting (`libcontainer/container_linux.go:406-421`).
- **Why it works:** it converts a race between error publication and cancellation effects into a fixed program order.
- **Copy when:** universally. This ordering rule is the single most repeated discipline in the study.
- **Anti-evidence:** ollama drops cleanup errors entirely (`server/sched.go:1372-1374`, `1705-1710`) — truthful but silent; repeated Kill failures would be invisible (`sources/ollama/server/sched.go` unload path).

### P5. Escalation ladders for external processes

- **Problem:** subprocesses ignore cooperative cancellation; teardown must escalate and always reap.
- **Demonstrated by:** docker-agent — SIGTERM to process group → 3s grace → SIGKILL → unconditional Wait, plus `cmd.WaitDelay=500ms` for grandchildren holding pipes (`pkg/tools/builtin/shell/shell.go:173-238`, `pkg/tools/builtin/shell/cmd_unix.go:14-25`). ollama's MLX client — SIGINT → 5s bounded wait → SIGKILL (`x/mlxrunner/client.go:137-154`). runc — hook waiter timer → Kill → still receive from the waiter channel ("deadline-with-join"), and `delete --force` polls Signal(0) up to ~10s (`libcontainer/configs/config.go:612-633`; `delete.go:17-26`).
- **Why it works:** escalation converts "process may hang" from a shutdown hazard into a bounded latency cost.
- **Copy when:** you ever hold a pid. Process groups (`Setpgid`) are required to reach grandchildren (both docker-agent and ollama's bash tool use them).
- **Anti-evidence:** ollama's llama-server path skips the ladder — SIGKILL then unbounded `<-s.done` — so one D-state subprocess wedges `unloadAllRunners` while it holds `loadedMu`, freezing the scheduler and making further SIGINT inert (`llm/llama_server.go:2569-2589`; `server/sched.go:1695-1711`; `server/routes.go:2021-2030`).

### P6. Buffered(1)/cap-1 result channels so abandoned consumers never strand producers

- **Problem:** producers delivering results to consumers that may give up must never block indefinitely.
- **Demonstrated by:** runc — `consoleC`, log-forwarder done chan, and BPF errChan all buffered size 1 (`utils_linux.go:116`; `libcontainer/logs/logs.go:18`; `libcontainer/seccomp/patchbpf/enosys_linux.go:157`). ollama — `successCh`/`errCh` capped 1 so late failures never block producers (`server/sched.go:189-190`). docker-agent — `listToolsWithTimeout` orphans the Tools() goroutine safely via buffered channel (`pkg/runtime/runtime.go:1925-1958`).
- **Why it works:** a capacity-1 buffer absorbs exactly the one message the producer owes a departed consumer.
- **Copy when:** results may outlive consumer interest — timeouts, cancellations, races.
- **Limitation:** it caps stranding at one message; it does not bound producer lifetime (see P7).

### P7. Escape-hatch selects guarding every send on consumer lifetime

- **Problem:** unbuffered sends inside callbacks or loops park goroutines forever when the consumer walks away — ollama's streaming producer parks inside `fn` on `ch <- res` after client disconnect, pinning the model's sole semaphore slot (`server/routes.go:649-792`; `llm/llama_server.go:1708`, `1542`); runc has four send-side leak sites (`notify_socket.go:133`; `events.go:77-86`; `libcontainer/notify_linux.go:57`; `libcontainer/process_linux.go:738`).
- **Demonstrated by:** temporal — LA result delivery selects between `laResultCh` and the workflow task's `doneCh` (`internal/internal_task_pollers.go:956-967`); poll→queue sends select against `stopCh` (`internal/internal_worker_base.go:835-847`). conc's Stream deposits an empty callback before re-panicking so the callbacker never blocks (`stream/stream.go:73-81`).
- **Why it works:** pairing each send with a done-channel branch ties producer lifetime to consumer lifetime instead of consumer politeness.
- **Copy when:** any send to a channel whose reader can disappear mid-flight. This is the direct fix for ollama's worst failure mode.

### P8. Adopt-or-reap plus published-stop handshakes for wedged operations

- **Problem:** operations raced against timeouts leave in-flight work whose ownership must transfer deterministically.
- **Demonstrated by:** docker-agent — startup-timeout leaves Connect running in `pendingConnect`; next Start adopts it, Stop reaps it; a single `adopted bool` guarantees exactly one fate (`pkg/tools/lifecycle/supervisor.go:302-402`). Its stop handshake publishes intent before contending for a lock; whoever releases the lock settles it, converting "can't acquire lock during shutdown" into guaranteed eventual stop (#4001, `pkg/tools/startable.go:562-586,621-646`).
- **Why it works:** abandoned-but-running becomes a first-class state with exactly one future owner, instead of a leak with a comment.
- **Copy when:** bounding any operation by racing a timer while the operation keeps running underneath.
- **Cost:** resource usage stays unbounded until the underlying attempt returns — accepted trade-off in docker-agent (`pkg/tools/startable.go:389-395`).

### P9. State machine with live reconciliation as mechanical idempotency

- **Problem:** repeated or concurrent shutdown calls must converge without double-executing side effects.
- **Demonstrated by:** runc — `Destroy()` dispatches through per-state guards, re-derives reality via `refreshState()` (freezer state, liveness, fifo presence), defends against PID reuse by comparing `/proc/<pid>` start times, and persists state atomically *before* the sync point so a killed creator leaves a deletable record (`libcontainer/state_linux.go:39-67,134-139,240-245`; `libcontainer/container_linux.go:882-906,919-952,997-1010` at `libcontainer/process_linux.go:997-1010`).
- **Why it works:** decisions re-validate against facts (kernel state) rather than trusting memory flags, surviving crashes and cross-process races.
- **Copy when:** shutdown spans crash boundaries or multiple independent actors.
- **Residual gap:** even this machine re-runs poststop hooks on repeated `Destroy()` because executed-hooks aren't recorded in state (`state_linux.go:64-66,104-106`) — reconciliation of *facts* must extend to reconciliation of *effects*.

### P10. Deterministic shutdown testing: virtual time, goleak, and cheap censuses

- **Problem:** shutdown races are timing-dependent and rarely reproduced by ordinary tests.
- **Demonstrated by:** temporal — `testing/synctest` verifies Stop returns after precisely `stopTimeout` (not the 70s poll bound) and that no task is dropped mid-shutdown (`internal/internal_worker_base_test.go:665-1127`); goleak sweeps with a targeted ignore for a known parked-coroutine frame, retried up to a minute (`test/integration_test.go:109-134`). ollama pins a real spinner leak with a `NumGoroutine` baseline assertion over 50 cycles (`progress/progress_test.go:49-73`). runc diffs `/proc/self/fd` before/after Run (`libcontainer/integration/exec_test.go:1693-1778`) — the right idea applied to the wrong resource.
- **Why it works:** virtual time makes deadlines testable in milliseconds; leak sweeps catch the goroutine nobody remembered to join.
- **Copy when:** immediately, at whichever tier fits — a NumGoroutine regression test is ~25 LOC and would have caught ollama's and runc's leak classes.
- **Anti-evidence:** three of five sources ship zero goroutine-leak instrumentation (`sources/conc/go.mod:5`; docker-agent and runc searches returned no matches).

### P11. Failure forensics before teardown

- **Problem:** destroying the environment destroys the evidence explaining the failure.
- **Demonstrated by:** runc reads OOM kill counts *before* killing init because systemd may remove the cgroup otherwise, then wraps the primary error with an OOM hint (`libcontainer/process_linux.go:789-821`). ollama's reaper converts the last stderr message into the surfaced error (`llm/llama_server.go:1021-1025`).
- **Why it works:** cleanup ordering determines diagnosability; evidence capture is a cleanup step, not a logging afterthought.
- **Copy when:** any teardown that removes state (cgroups, processes, temp dirs) needed to interpret the failure.

### P12. Recover–act–rethrow wrappers making deferred side effects part of panic propagation

- **Problem:** panic paths skip ordinary control flow, stranding collaborators (blocked consumers, uncanceled siblings).
- **Demonstrated by:** conc — `ContextPool.Go` recovers, cancels, re-panics (`pool/context_pool.go:26-36`); `Stream.Go` deposits an empty callback before re-panicking so the consumer never blocks (`stream/stream.go:73-81`). temporal transports recovered panics across goroutines with stacks (mirrored in ollama's mlxthread package, `x/internal/mlxthread/thread.go:33-44,181-191`).
- **Why it works:** it preserves panic semantics while guaranteeing the panic's collateral damage is repaired.
- **Copy when:** goroutines whose panic can deadlock peers. Combine with first-wins CAS storage so secondary panics never overwrite the first (`panics/panics.go:29`).

## Key Differences

**Where the cancellation backbone lives.** Three sources thread `context.Context` from an entry point down to blocking operations (docker-agent from `signal.NotifyContext` through per-turn cancels to leaf selects; temporal from user-suppliable roots through `WithCancelCause` registries; ollama from gin handlers through the scheduler into subprocess health polls). runc is the deliberate outlier: contexts are nearly absent (`context.Background()` at `sources/runc/main.go:184`; one genuine `WithTimeout` in a mount helper at `libcontainer/process_linux.go:692`) because the cancelled entity is a process tree, negotiated through signal forwarding and kernel primitives (pidfd, cgroup.kill). ollama is a hybrid: contexts govern Go-side waiting; SIGKILL governs subprocesses. The lesson isn't "always contexts" — it's that the backbone must reach whatever actually blocks, and for external processes nothing cooperative reaches them at all.

**Who waits, and how hard that question bites.** temporal concentrates the answer: one `baseWorker` join point covers every runtime goroutine, with exceptions enumerated and externally bounded (`internal/internal_task_pollers.go:1059-1093` detached LA execution). conc concentrates it in a type. docker-agent distributes it by goroutine class with explicit assignments — stream goroutines joined implicitly by channel drain, background agents by `Handler.StopAll` + `wg.Wait()`, toolsets by the embedder's 30s budget — and honestly documents the deliberately unwaited ones (`pkg/tools/startable.go:389-395`). runc splits per-subsystem and delegates the remainder to process exit. ollama simply lacks an answer for a whole class: streaming producers (`server/routes.go:1133-1160,1185-1215` show the same shape in pull/push/create handlers). Distribution is fine when each class has an owner; silence is not distribution.

**Grace-window policy.** None (conc — cooperative only), opt-in-with-default-zero (temporal — `WorkerStopTimeout=0` collapses the window its public docs promise, `internal/worker.go:291-294` vs `internal/activity.go:274-282`), fixed budgets (docker-agent — 30s toolsets, 3s process grace, 500ms pipes), bounded polls (runc — 10s forced-delete, 30s sd_notify barrier, 1-minute mount deadline), or absent-on-primary-path (ollama). Temporal's mismatch between documentation and default is the cautionary version: a promised grace window that defaults off is worse than honestly having none.

**Idempotency mechanism spectrum.** Strongest to weakest: runc's reconciling state machine (mechanical, crash-surviving, but effect-recording incomplete); docker-agent's layered CAS/Once/handshake per component (mechanical within each, no global story); temporal's atomic pieces under a racy top (`dispatcherImpl.closed` flag, heartbeat CAS, refcount pinning — but check-then-close at `AggregatedWorker.Stop`); ollama's layered guards on bookkeeping with abrupt overall shutdown; conc's pure ownership discipline (zero mechanism; double-close panic available to any second caller). Notably, maturity does not predict this ordering — the 8/10 SDK shares the 6/10 library's exact defect class.

**Test discipline.** temporal (synctest virtual time + goleak + integration shutdown suites) > docker-agent (issue-numbered regression tests per lifecycle bug: #2872, #3069/#3070, #3200, #3584, #4001/#4004) > ollama (one NumGoroutine test, scheduler shutdown tests) > runc (fd-leak and behavioral kill tests, no goroutine census) > conc (semantics well tested, leaks and concurrency of shutdown untested). The correlation with rating is imperfect — docker-agent reaches 9 without goleak through sheer regression-test coverage — but the bottom half of the table is uniformly the least-tested half.

## Tradeoffs

**Graceful drain vs fast teardown.** Temporal's drain mode blocks polls up to 70s so the server completes them gracefully and no polled task is dropped (`internal/internal_task_pollers.go:305-343`); its legacy mode returns instantly but abandons the poll goroutine. Ollama chose the opposite pole: `srvr.Close()` on SIGTERM gives instant teardown but clients get connection resets instead of a final error frame (`server/routes.go:2026`). Best-fit depends on whether the peer expects a terminal protocol message (drain) or tolerates resets (abandon). Failure mode of drain: a wedged in-flight poll delays Stop by the full cap — hence temporal's `WorkerStopTimeout` outer bound.

**Mechanical vs contractual idempotency.** Runc's state machine survives crashes and cross-process racing but paid in complexity and still misses effect-recording (hook double-fire). Conc pays zero runtime cost but a second `Wait` caller panics on double-close (`pool/pool.go:75`). Docker-agent's middle path — per-component `sync.Once`/CAS plus one published-stop handshake — suggests the pragmatic optimum: mechanical guards exactly where concurrency exposure exists, ownership rules where it doesn't.

**Detachment benefits vs reattachment obligations.** Docker-agent detaches connections for reuse across requests; the price is that every interactive operation inside the detached region must remember to observe the stashed cancellable parent — forgetting deadlocks until the connection dies, which is precisely what happened before the `cancellable_parent.go` pattern was introduced (#documented incident, `pkg/tools/mcp/cancellable_parent.go:31-49`). Ollama's refcounted downloads buy resilience across client churn at the cost of disk/network work no request can see or cancel except by starting-and-dropping a watcher (`server/download.go:512-513`). Temporal exits stuck coroutines unwaited, guaranteeing dispatcher close at the price of deferred user cleanup interleaving arbitrarily with eviction (`internal/internal_workflow.go:1365-1381`). Detachment is correct everywhere it appears; every source's incidents cluster at the reattachment seams.

**Enforcement investment vs review discipline.** Temporal's battery (synctest + goleak + integration suites) catches shutdown regressions deterministically but costs test complexity and needs curated ignore lists for known-benign frames (`test/integration_test.go:114-133`). Docker-agent achieves similar confidence through issue-numbered regression tests alone, but its own analysis notes a future detached goroutine without a stop path would not be caught mechanically. Below that bar, leaks are invisible: conc, ollama (mostly), and runc discovered their leak classes only because this study looked for them.

**Backpressure shape.** Conc ties queue capacity to pool width (`MaxGoroutines()+1`, `stream/stream.go:110-117`) so backpressure surfaces as blocking submit, never unbounded memory — but a saturated pool cannot be abandoned (no ctx-aware submit, `pool/pool.go:54-66`). Temporal's 100,000-slot LA tunnel buys throughput and simplicity at the cost of effective backpressure removal (`internal/internal_task_pollers.go:260-284`). Ollama's unbuffered progress channels give natural backpressure against slow clients but couple producer life to consumer politeness — the exact trade that produced its producer-park leak. Bounded-and-abandonable (select-guarded sends, P7) dominates both extremes.

## Decision Guide

**Building a concurrency primitive/library?** Start from conc's shape — one owning WaitGroup type, panics republished at join, config frozen after first use — then fix its three demonstrated gaps before shipping: wrap the close-and-join sequence in a `sync.Once` for concurrent-callers safety (its `pool.go:75` double-close), accept contexts in submission (`TryGo`/`GoCtx` selecting on done alongside the limiter), and adopt goleak in your own tests. Nothing else in this study is more liftable.

**Building a long-lived supervisor of many mixed lifetimes?** Follow docker-agent: partition lifetimes at the boundary (request vs connection via `WithoutCancel`), centralize restart/reconnect in one supervisor with adopt-or-reap semantics, publish stop requests before contending for locks, and bound every teardown (grace ladders for processes, budgeted stop chains). Add its habit of pinning every lifecycle bug with an issue-numbered regression test. Its known debt — unenforced fire-and-forget contracts — argues for adding the goleak sweep it currently lacks.

**Supervising external processes?** The ladder is mandatory, not optional: group-signal (SIGTERM/SIGINT) → timed grace → SIGKILL → unconditional Wait, with `Setpgid` to reach grandchildren and `WaitDelay` for pipe-holding descendants (docker-agent `shell.go:173-238`; ollama `agent/tools/bash_unix.go:24-49`). Never wait on a reaper channel without a deadline (ollama's `llama_server.go:2579-2585` is the counterexample), and never perform that wait while holding a lock other subsystems need (`sched.go:1695-1711` compounds it). Keep a second-signal escape hatch in the signal loop — no studied source has one; ollama's analysis recommends it and the wedge scenario it prevents is demonstrated in-repo.

**Short-lived CLI?** Runc proves process exit legitimately reclaims detached loops and unbounded drains; copy its ordered LIFO defer chains, buffered(1) result channels, forensics-before-teardown, and state-machine destroy. But if any part ships as a library (as libcontainer does), export a Join/Shutdown handle aggregating your goroutines — runc's analysis explicitly notes embedders are left to implement reaping themselves (`libcontainer/container_linux.go:433-437`).

**Choosing an idempotency mechanism:** count the actors who can trigger shutdown. One (single-owner rule, enforced by review): conc's discipline suffices if you accept the panic blast radius. Few, in-process: `sync.Once` or CAS per component (docker-agent, temporal's inner pieces). Many or cross-process, or crash-resume matters: reconciling state machine (runc) — and record executed side effects (hooks, closes) in that state, the gap runc left.

**When to invest in shutdown testing:** the moment you have more than one goroutine family or any detached goroutine. Minimum viable tier is a NumGoroutine-baseline regression test (ollama's `progress_test.go:53-73` template); full tier is temporal's synctest + goleak. Runc's fd-leak harness shows the right technique misapplied — assert on the resource that actually leaks.

## Practical Tips

- Make channel close the only authoritative "done"; treat every intermediate event as droppable decoration, and defer the close so it fires on early returns and panics (docker-agent `finalizeEventChannel`).
- Fix the order: record the causal outcome → trigger cancellation → publish. Repeat it in comments; test it with sibling-cancel scenarios (conc's `context_pool.go:39-47` is the canonical 8-line version).
- Use `context.WithCancelCause` from day one and branch on `context.Cause` — retrofitting causes onto sentinel channels is the refactor temporal avoided by starting there.
- Give every result-delivery channel a capacity-1 buffer minimum, and pair every send with a select against the consumer's done channel. Together these eliminate the entire producer-park leak class (P6+P7).
- Assign every goroutine family an owner object at creation, and put the assignment in a comment next to the spawn — temporal documents each channel's creation/closure sites on the struct (`internal/internal_worker.go:91-98,1279-1282`); docker-agent annotates watcher lifetimes ("must outlive ctx", `supervisor.go:289`).
- Bound every external kill and always Wait after the last signal, regardless of timeout — the Wait is how zombies stay impossible (both docker-agent's ladder and runc's deadline-with-join do this; ollama's MLX client shows the intended shape inside the same repo whose llama-server path omits it).
- Pin every lifecycle bug you fix with a regression test named after the issue — docker-agent's suite (#2872…#4004) is the study's best defense-in-depth example.
- Add a second-SIGINT-forced-exit hatch to any long-running process's signal loop, and keep cleanup off the signal goroutine's stack so a wedged cleanup can't disable further handling (ollama's wedge demonstrates both needs).
- Write the NumGoroutine leak test now; it is ~25 lines and catches the majority of leak classes found in this study.
- When racing an operation against a timeout, decide the abandoned attempt's fate explicitly — adopt it, reap it, or let it finish under its own bound — and encode that decision in a single flag (docker-agent's `pendingConnect` adoption).

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **`close()` reachable from more than one call path.** Conc's per-call `close(p.tasks)` and temporal's check-then-close `stopC` are the same bug at different scales; both analyses derived the double-close panic from code reading and neither has a test exercising it. Caution sign: a close not wrapped in Once/CAS/state-guard.
- **Unbuffered sends whose reader can disappear.** Ollama's producer parked inside a completion callback (pinning a semaphore slot), runc's four send-leak sites. Caution sign: `ch <- x` inside a callback or event loop without a paired `done` select.
- **Cleanup that waits unboundedly while holding a contended lock.** Ollama's unload-under-`loadedMu` turns one stuck subprocess into a frozen scheduler and inert Ctrl-C. Caution sign: any `<-done` between Lock and Unlock on a shared lock.
- **Signal handling that dies after first use.** Ollama's one-shot handler with a size-1 buffered channel and exited reader. Caution sign: no second-signal escape hatch in a server/CLI process.
- **Conflating cancellation causes in error messages.** Ollama reports client disconnects as "timed out waiting for llama-server to start" (`llm/llama_server.go:1300-1302`) — misleading diagnostics that cost debugging time. Also its token-repeat abort returns `ctx.Err()` while the context is live (`1690-1701`). Caution sign: `ctx.Err()` returned or logged where the actual trigger was different.
- **Documented grace windows that defaults disable.** Temporal's `GetWorkerStopChannel` docs promise wait-then-cancel sequencing; `WorkerStopTimeout=0` makes the wait vanish silently (`internal/activity.go:274-282` vs `internal/worker.go:291-294`). Caution sign: a timeout knob defaulting to zero beneath a doc that assumes nonzero.
- **Side effects re-fired on retry.** Runc's repeated `Destroy()` re-runs poststop hooks. Caution sign: an idempotency story covering resources but not effects.
- **`os.Exit` on a hot path above defers.** Runc's attached-tty SIGINT handler resets the console and exits, skipping container destroy and IO drain (`tty.go:145-151`). Caution sign: exit calls reachable from signal handlers without a cleanup pass.
- **Untracked tickers and fire-and-forget helpers.** Runc's `time.Tick` ticker parks forever after its consumer exits (`events.go:77-86`); temporal's `InterruptCh` leaks its forwarder for process life if the channel is discarded (`internal/internal_utils.go:183-196`). Caution sign: goroutines started without registration on any WaitGroup or registry, justified only by process lifetime.
- **Reuse semantics differing silently across sibling types.** Conc's `Pool` resets for reuse, `Stream` doesn't (post-Wait reuse panics on closed queue), `ContextPool` reuse runs on a canceled context (`pool.go:77-79` vs `stream/stream.go:69,97`; `context_pool.go:56`). Caution sign: a family of similar types whose post-completion behavior must be memorized per type.
- **Sleep-based workarounds in ordering tests.** Both conc's and temporal's suites contain sleeps or retry windows around cancellation ordering — honest about raciness, but a sign the ordering isn't yet deterministic. Caution sign: `time.Sleep` guarding an ordering assertion.

## Notable Absences

Evidence none of the five sources provides, relevant to Aren:

- **No observable "cancel requested, work still running" state machine.** The closest approximations are partial: docker-agent offers `WaitStopped`/`?wait=true` bounded polling after immediate DeleteSession return (`pkg/server/session_manager.go:853-919`); temporal logs "graceful stop timed out" and proceeds (`internal/internal_worker_base.go:913-923`); runc refuses `Destroy` with `ErrRunning` until verified stopped (`state_linux.go:134-139`). None exposes a first-class Canceling state. If Aren's PRD requires one, it must be designed fresh.
- **Second-signal forced-exit escape hatch: absent in all five.** Recommended by ollama's own analysis; the wedge it prevents is demonstrated in ollama's code.
- **Repo-wide goroutine-leak enforcement in one of five sources** (temporal). Nobody aggregates detached work into a joinable handle — docker-agent's analysis flags background jobs as lacking an independent enumeration in the Close path, and runc/libcontainer provides no aggregate handle to embedders at all.
- **Cancellation-aware submission in the library-tier source.** Conc cannot abandon a saturated pool; no studied library primitive demonstrates `GoCtx`-style submit, so Aren would be designing it from first principles (conc's analysis sketches it as its top future consideration).
- **Windows process-group kill semantics verified nowhere.** Docker-agent confirmed platform-split files exist but read only the Unix path.
- **Aren PRD documents unread by all five analyses.** Each report independently states `Aren/docs/phase-1-prd/02-lifecycle-requirements.md` was inaccessible under source-isolation rules. All Aren-alignment statements here rest on the injected dimension text; PRD-imposed guarantees (e.g., max cancel-to-terminal latency, shutdown idempotency requirements) remain unverified.

## Per-Source Notes

Brief anchors; the synthesis above carries the comparative weight.

- **conc (6/10):** Stdlib-only structured-concurrency toolkit. Best-in-study ownership primitive (one raw spawn site, panic republishing at join, error-before-cancel ordering, recover–act–rethrow wrappers). Weakest cancellation surface (opt-in ContextPool only, no timeouts, blocked submitters cannot escape) and zero leak instrumentation; concurrent `Wait` double-closes. Highest density of directly liftable micro-mechanisms (~15–50 LOC each).
- **docker-agent (9/10):** Interactive agent application with the study's most complete lifecycle architecture: two-lifetime contexts, centralized supervisor with adopt/reap, published-stop handshakes, grace ladders, conversational cancellation, and issue-numbered regression tests for every lifecycle bug. Deductions are for reliance on documented-but-unenforced goroutine contracts and absence of a leak harness.
- **ollama (6/10):** Model server with disciplined mid-layer plumbing (scheduler channels, PID-matched idempotent unload, refcounted downloads, VRAM-convergence gating, exemplary mlxthread worker package) but failed edges: streaming producers park on unbuffered sends pinning semaphore slots, unbounded final reap wedges the scheduler and signal handling under a lock, abrupt SIGTERM, dropped cleanup errors. Contains both the study's best and worst external-process teardown in the same binary.
- **runc (6/10):** Container CLI demonstrating that signal/kernel-driven supervision can be rigorous: truthful status-after-flush publication, `%w; %w` error merging, reconciling state machine with PID-reuse defense, forensics-before-teardown, ordered LIFO defers. Cooperative cancellation is essentially absent; several unguarded sends and the never-closed epoller rely on process exit; poststop hooks double-fire on retry.
- **temporal-sdk-go (8/10):** Workflow SDK with the study's most engineered shutdown: staged top-down stop sequences encoding semantics in close order, cause-typed cancellation registries keyed by task token, drain modes, background-rooted result publication, and the only deterministic shutdown test battery (synctest + goleak). Residual flaws are precise and instructive: the topmost `Stop()` is the one non-atomic link, the default stop timeout contradicts documented grace, and a few detached goroutines are bounded but never joined.

## Open Questions

1. **Does gin's `c.Stream` actually abandon reads on client disconnect?** Ollama's producer-park severity rests on repo-side evidence plus inferred CloseNotify semantics (module cache unavailable, `go.mod:8` pins gin v1.10.0); a targeted disconnect experiment measuring NumGoroutine and slot occupancy would confirm.
2. **Do concurrent `Wait`/`Stop` callers actually panic in practice?** Derived from code reading in both conc (`pool.go:75`) and temporal (`internal_worker.go:1616-1640`); neither suite exercises concurrent shutdown. Both analyses recommend the same experiment and fix.
3. **What grace-window default should Aren choose?** Temporal's default-zero-vs-documented-grace mismatch and docker-agent's fixed budgets are the two data points; the unread PRD presumably constrains this and could not be consulted.
4. **Does temporal's heartbeat batching progress-loss race matter in production?** Acknowledged in-code (`internal_task_handlers.go:2227-2232`) with no regression test pinning observable behavior.
5. **How does runc arbitrate concurrent `delete --force` across processes?** Filesystem state reconciliation appears sufficient, but no flock/lockfile was found; cross-invocation racing was analyzed statically only.
6. **Should Aren adopt drain-to-completion or abandon-on-stop for its executor?** Temporal shows both modes coexisting behind a capability switch — the open question is whether Aren's Phase 2 "failure laboratory" needs the drain mode's no-task-dropped guarantee from the start, or legacy-mode simplicity first.
