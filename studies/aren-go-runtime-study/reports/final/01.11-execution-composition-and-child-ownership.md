## Executive Summary

Phase 12 composition asks a deceptively narrow question — how does a parent own children when execution may be sequential, fail-fast parallel, collect-all, or bounded-pool — and each source answers it at a different scale. conc answers it as a library: a flat `Pool`/`WaitGroup` owner with builder-type composition helpers that preserve limiter, error-policy, and generic result type rather than erasing to an interface, deterministic submission-ordered aggregation, and explicit opt-in sibling cancellation (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:78-82`). docker-agent answers it as a product: two concrete delegation paths — `runForwarding` for blocking, event-forwarding `transfer_task`/`fork-skill` and `runCollecting` for detached `run_background_agent` with caps (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:340-412`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:33-40`) — linked by `ParentID`/`DelegationLineage` identity but with no generic pool, ordered collection, or panic propagation. temporal-sdk-go answers it as a durable runtime: server-enforced `ParentClosePolicy` plus a deterministic coroutine dispatcher with low-level primitives (`Future`/`Channel`/`Selector`/`WaitGroup`/`Semaphore` at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:54-79`) and expects workflow code to hand-roll aggregation, cancellation, and ordering.

The convergent lesson is that composition quality is decided by three choices made explicit: where the terminal barrier sits relative to child cleanup, whether sibling cancellation is implicit or opt-in, and how result ordering is enforced. conc and temporal agree that a parent is not terminal until a `Wait`/`Complete` barrier passes; docker-agent deliberately violates this for background children via `context.WithoutCancel` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`). All three agree that sibling failure should not cancel siblings by default — but only conc makes the alternative an explicit, typed policy (`WithCancelOnError`/`WithFailFast`). Only conc provides submission-ordered results out of the box.

For Aren, the importable core is small: conc's slot-reservation aggregator + limiter channel + builder preservation, temporal's dual-future/handle distinction and policy-driven close, and docker-agent's lineage isolation and session-scoped event attributability. The bulk to avoid is conc's reusability-after-Wait, docker-agent's outlive-by-default detached context, and temporal's hand-rolled aggregation that leaves ordering to caller discipline.

## Core Thesis

**Composition is a typed policy over ownership, not a generic executor.** Every source that handles Phase 12 well separates two concerns that are often conflated: *who owns the child* (identity, lifecycle, resource sharing) and *how the set completes* (fail-fast vs collect-all, ordered vs completion-ordered, bounded vs unbounded). When those two are collapsed into one `Go()` method, the design silently chooses defaults for cancellation, ordering, and terminal semantics that callers cannot change without forking the type.

Three archetypes emerge along that separation:

- conc's "flat builder" — ownership is always `Pool -> goroutine` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/waitgroup.go:22-25`), and every variation in *how* the set completes is a new concrete type produced by a `With*` builder that copies the limiter and generic parameter rather than erasing it (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:118-148`). Strength: callers pick semantics by picking a type. Weakness: nesting requires manual propagation and there is no hierarchical identity.
- docker-agent's "two-path delegation" — ownership is `Session -> SubSession` via `ParentID`/`DelegationLineage` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:422-433`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:115-275`), and *how* differs by product need: forwarding (blocking, `swapCurrentAgent`, `RunStream` drain) vs collecting (detached, `PinAgent`, token-usage only). Strength: concrete executors keep TUI/pinned-agent semantics intact. Weakness: no abstraction for the general case — "run 5 helpers, limit 3 concurrent, collect ordered, fail-fast" has no spelling.
- temporal's "durable command machine + cooperative dispatcher" — ownership is server-persisted via `StartChildWorkflowExecutionCommandAttributes` with `ParentClosePolicy` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_event_handlers.go:565-648`) and local coroutines multiplexed on `dispatcherImpl` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:163-180`). Strength: parent/child survives worker crashes with replay determinism. Weakness: composition leaves ordering, fail-fast, and bounded concurrency to user code using `Selector`/`Semaphore` primitives.

Aren's Phase 12 sits between conc and temporal: it needs conc's explicit type-per-policy and submission-order guarantee, temporal's hierarchical identity with server- or supervisor-enforced close policy, and docker-agent's lesson that preserving concrete executor semantics (tool set, agent pinning, budget sharing) matters more than abstracting to a single `Executor` interface.

## Approach Models

**conc — Flat builder pool.** Ownership is `owner -> goroutine` via `Go()` and a barrier `Wait()` — there is no task tree, no `ChildID`, no lifecycle events. The WaitGroup struct owns a `sync.WaitGroup` plus a `panics.Catcher` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/waitgroup.go:22-25`), and `Pool.Wait` closes the `tasks` channel then blocks on `handle.Wait` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:72-82`). Bounded parallelism is a `chan struct{}` limiter with `limit()`/`release()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:164-174`) and lazy worker creation via a `tasks chan func()` handoff (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:38-66`). Composition variants are separate concrete types produced by `WithErrors`, `WithContext`, `WithResults`, `WithMaxGoroutines` that shallow-copy the limiter and preserve generic `T` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:118-148`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:55-71`). Result determinism is by slot reservation (`resultAggregator.nextIndex/save/collect`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:96-141`) with a documented "order is guaranteed to be submission order" contract. Best for callers who are synchronous and own the whole scope; weakest where hierarchical identity or terminal-once enforcement is required (pools are reusable after `Wait` resets `initOnce` at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:79`).

**docker-agent — Delegation, not a pool.** Ownership is modeled as sessions: `Session.ParentID`/`IsSubSession()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:422-425`) plus `DelegationLineage []string` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:426-433`) checked by `validateDelegation` at depth 10 with cycle detection (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:60-85`). Construction is centralized in `SubSessionConfig`/`newSubSession` (15 fields, lineage snapshot, permission/tool-filter merge at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:115-275`) but execution diverges: `runForwarding` blocks the parent and optionally `swapCurrentAgent` while draining `RunStream` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:340-412`); `runCollecting` detaches via `context.WithoutCancel` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`), pins `AgentName`, and persists via `persistBackgroundSubSession`. Bounded parallelism is admission control (`maxConcurrentTasks=20`, `maxTotalTasks=100` at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:33-40`) checked before spawn, not a semaphore that queues. This model preserves TUI/pinned-agent semantics and is heavily tested for lineage isolation and `currentAgent` handling (issue #3886), but provides no `errgroup`-like composition — `errgroup.WithContext`+`SetLimit` appears only for RAG embedding (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/rag/embed/embed.go:120-122`), never for child agents.

**temporal-sdk-go — Durable parent-child with cooperative concurrency.** Two ownership layers: server-side `StartChildWorkflowExecutionCommandAttributes` carrying `ParentClosePolicy`/`WaitForCancellation`/`RetryPolicy` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow.go:441-569`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_event_handlers.go:565-648`) with a `childWorkflowCommandStateMachine` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:79-83`), and local coroutines on `dispatcherImpl` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:163-180`) driven by `ExecuteUntilAllBlocked`. Primitives are `futureImpl`/`selectorImpl`/`waitGroupImpl`/`semaphoreImpl` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:54-79`). A `childWorkflowFutureImpl` holds both a result future and an execution future (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:242-245`) so start confirmation and result are separate (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:475-489`). There is no `Gather` or `Pool(n)` helper — bounded parallelism is manual `Semaphore.Acquire` via `Await(size-cur>=n)` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:2045-2054`), and ordered results require iterating futures sequentially. Parent terminal is eventually consistent: parent `Complete` then server applies `ABANDON`/`TERMINATE`/`REQUEST_CANCEL` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow_testsuite.go:1222-1237`); local `dispatcher.Close` `Goexit`s remaining coroutines (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:1365-1381`). Fits durable orchestration where child survival after parent terminal is a policy, not a bug.

## Pattern Catalog

### P1. Builder-chain type conversion that preserves concrete executor semantics

- **Problem:** abstracting varied composition needs into one `Executor` or `any` erases limiter config, error policy, and generic result type, forcing callers to re-prove ordering and boundedness at each call site.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc most explicitly — `Pool.deref` shallow-copies the `limiter` chan (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:118-133`), then `WithErrors()->ErrorPool`, `WithContext()->ContextPool`, `ResultPool.WithErrors()->ResultErrorPool`, etc. (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:140-148`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:55-71`) keep the boundedness guarantee and `T` across conversions. docker-agent shows the same instinct without the types: `SubSessionConfig` preserves `Permissions`, `SafetyPolicy`, `AllowedTools` per child (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:132-168`) so delegation keeps tool semantics. temporal shows the counterpoint: `WorkflowOutboundInterceptor.ExecuteChildWorkflow` delegates to `Next` without erasing per-child `dataConverter`/`failureConverter` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/interceptor.go:241-243`).
- **Why it works:** callers select semantics by selecting a type (`ContextPool` vs `ErrorPool` vs `ResultContextPool`), and the compiler enforces that the chosen semantics travel with the handle.
- **When to copy:** whenever the pool/group abstraction has orthogonal axes (error vs result vs context vs limit) — make each axis a concrete type produced by a builder, not a flag on one type.
- **When overkill:** scope has exactly one shape (e.g., pure worker pool with no results) — a single `Pool` type suffices.
- **Evidence:** conc's `WithMaxGoroutines` panic-if-initialized guard (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:111-114`) forces configuration before first `Go`, keeping the `deref` copy honest; `ResultContextPool` panic test verifies two siblings are cancelled through the preserved context (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_context_pool_test.go:121-142`).

### P2. Slot-reservation deterministic aggregation (submission-ordered results)

- **Problem:** concurrent children complete out of order, but callers need results keyed to inputs (prompt N → result N) without sorting after the fact.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc only as a provided helper — `ResultPool.Go` reserves `idx:=agg.nextIndex()` under mutex before dispatch (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:32-37`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:96-103`), `save(idx, f(), false)` writes to that slot, `collect` returns the slice in index order with stably sorted `errored` filtering (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:123-141`). `Stream` applies the same idea: per-task `callbackCh` queued in submission order, single `callbacker` goroutine drains sequentially (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:18-32`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:121-138`). Temporal and docker-agent lack this: temporal's `WaitGroup` example appends in completion order (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow_test.go:1100-1102`) and `Selector.AddFuture` fires first ready in registration order (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:1444-1558`); docker-agent's `HandleList` iterates a `concurrent.Map` in random order (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:448-456`).
- **Why it works:** index reservation is contention only at submission (one mutex increment), not at completion, and ordering requires no channel sorting or extra synchronization.
- **When to copy:** any collect-all where results must line up with inputs — parallel tool calls, fan-out subagents, batched embeddings. Document as conc does: "order is guaranteed to be submission order" (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:23-24`).
- **Risk:** `WithCollectErrored=false` silently drops values for errored indices (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_error_pool.go:35-39`); callers needing partial results must opt in via `WithCollectErrored` or lose data.

### P3. Lazy worker reuse with channel limiter for bounded parallelism

- **Problem:** unbounded fan-out exhausts goroutines; pre-spawning workers wastes resources when demand is low; backpressure must be explicit.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc — `Pool.Go` first tries non-blocking `p.tasks <- f`; only on `default` or limiter availability does it spawn a worker (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:42-66`); workers loop `for f:=range tasks { f() }` with `defer limiter.release()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:150-162`). The `limiter` is literally `type limiter chan struct{}` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:164-174`) with no-op `release` when nil, so bounded and unbounded share one code path. `iter.Iterator.ForEachIdx` farms via `atomic.Int64` over `MaxGoroutines` capped to `len(input)` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/iter/iter.go:59-85`) — a second bounded form in the same repo. Temporal provides the primitive (`semaphoreImpl.Acquire` via `Await`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:2045-2054`) but no pool wrapper; docker-agent uses admission caps that reject instead of queue (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:319-329`).
- **Why it works:** zero-cost creation, goroutine count equals concurrent demand bounded by limit, blocking `Go` provides backpressure without a separate queue.
- **Copy when:** pool lifetime is short or demand is bursty; blocking submission (rather than `TryGo`) is acceptable because the limit is the backpressure signal.
- **Cost:** blocking `Go` can stall the submitter when `MaxGoroutines=1` with no `ctx`-aware `TryGo`; conc documents ~1 µs teardown overhead that makes it unsuitable for very short tasks (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:26-29`).

### P4. Explicit fail-fast vs collect-all policy

- **Problem:** conflating error collection with sibling cancellation hides the policy; callers cannot tell whether `errors.Join` includes `context.Canceled` noise or the first real error.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc — `ErrorPool.Wait` returns `errors.Join(errs...)` unless `onlyFirstError` then `errs[0]` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/error_pool.go:35-48`); `ContextPool` cancels siblings only when `WithCancelOnError` is set (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:78-82`), with `WithFailFast = WithFirstError+WithCancelOnError` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:87-92`). The `addErr`-before-`cancel` ordering is deliberately "leaky" to preserve `WithFirstError` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:40-43`). Temporal has no Group flag — sibling cancellation requires sharing a `cancelCtx` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/context.go:181-325`) and fail-fast must be hand-coded via `Select`+`cancel()`. docker-agent is isolated-by-default with no flag at all: each background task gets its own `cancel` from `context.WithCancel(WithoutCancel(ctx))` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`), and `HandleRun` failures affect only that `taskID`.
- **Why it works:** policy is visible at the construction site; tests can assert the two branches — without `WithCancelOnError` sibling completes and lacks `context.Canceled` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool_test.go:149-166`), with it sibling observes `ctx.Done()` and join includes `Canceled` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool_test.go:134-147`).
- **Copy:** always separate `Collect(errors.Join)` from `FailFast(first + cancel)`; make the combinator explicit (as `WithFailFast`) and test both.

### P5. Delegation identity: ParentID + lineage + depth/cycle guards

- **Problem:** without first-class child identity, events and results cannot be attributed, cycles go undetected, and sibling fan-out shares mutable lineage slices.
- **Demonstrated by:** docker-agent most thoroughly — `ParentID`/`WithParentID`/`IsSubSession()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:422-425`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:1621-1623`), `DelegationLineage []string` with snapshot accessor (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/session.go:426-433`), `maxDelegationDepth=10` and `validateDelegation` that clones the lineage slice to avoid sharing backing arrays (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:60-85`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation_test.go:744-754`). conc has no identity beyond a result slot index (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:96-103`), which could be adapted into a `ChildID`. temporal's identity is server-enforced `ChildWorkflowOptions.WorkflowID` plus `WorkflowInfo` parent/root pointers (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow.go:441-569`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow.go:1512-1583`).
- **Copy when:** nesting depth >1 or parent attribution matters for observability; clone slices eagerly (as `validateDelegation` does) to prevent sibling interference.

### P6. Attributable events without a global bus

- **Problem:** a single event channel tempts fan-out via filtering; child events then lose determinism or leak across parents.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc — per-task channels in `Stream` recycled via `sync.Pool` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:142-154`) and per-slot `resultAggregator` isolation avoid a global bus. docker-agent — `SessionScoped.GetSessionID()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/event.go:18-22`) plus `PersistenceObserver` filtering (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/persistence_observer.go:74-79`) and `OnBackgroundEvent` dedicated sink for `TokenUsage` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/runtime.go:179-184`) keep child events attributable. temporal — per-workflow `commandsHelper` queues and per-signal channel maps (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_command_state_machine.go:151-169`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:209-211`) with `Selector` fan-in (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:130-145`).
- **Copy:** prefer per-child attribution (channel, slot, `SessionID` tag) over a shared bus; provide a `Selector`-like typed multiplexer for fan-in rather than `reflect.Select`.

### P7. Dual-future handle pattern (start vs result)

- **Problem:** callers need to signal/cancel a child after it starts but before it completes; a single `Future[T]` conflates the two.
- **Demonstrated by:** temporal — `childWorkflowFutureImpl` holds `executionFuture` (handle) + `decodeFutureImpl` (result) (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:242-245`); `GetChildWorkflowExecution().Get` returns the handle separately from `Future.Get` for the result (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:475-489`). conc has no handle — result and lifecycle share the `Wait` return; docker-agent exposes handles as `taskID` strings for background tasks (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:443-464`).
- **Copy when:** children are long-lived enough that callers will interact mid-flight (cancel, heartbeat, re-attach). Even in-process, a `Handle` type with `Cancel()`/`ID()` separate from `Result()` pays off.

### P8. Wait barrier as the only terminal gate

- **Problem:** parents that "finish" while children are still mutating shared state create torn results, missed cleanup, or use-after-terminal writes.
- **Demonstrated by:** conc enforces by blocking — `WaitGroup.Wait` blocks on `sync.WaitGroup` then `Repanic` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/waitgroup.go:38-43`), `Pool.Wait` closes `tasks` then waits workers drain `range p.tasks` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:72-82`), `Stream.Wait` waits `pool` then `callbackerHandle` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:91-103`). temporal enforces via protocol — `syncWorkflowDefinition.Execute` sets result then `executeDispatcher` calls `env.Complete` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:692-734`); `ParentClosePolicy` determines whether children are abandoned or terminated (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow_testsuite.go:1222-1237`). docker-agent shows the negative case: background children intentionally outlive the parent stream (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`) and mutate `parent.AddLiveSubSession`/`persistBackgroundSubSession` after `StreamStopped` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:498-521`).
- **Copy:** choose a policy explicitly — terminal-after-cleanup (barrier) or terminal-with-outlive (policy) — and make `WaitForChildren`/close-policy a visible construction parameter rather than an accident of `WithoutCancel`.

## Key Differences

**Hierarchy vs flat.** conc is single-level by design: every goroutine is owned by the `Pool`/`WaitGroup` that spawned it (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/waitgroup.go:22-25`); nesting requires threading a `*WaitGroup` manually (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/README.md:67-76`). docker-agent and temporal are hierarchical: docker-agent via `ParentID`/`DelegationLineage`/`setParentIDs` recursively re-parenting branched sessions (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/session/branch.go:381-391`), temporal via server `ParentClosePolicy` + local coroutine tree under `dispatcherImpl`. The divergence reflects library vs application vs platform constraints: a stdlib-only library cannot justify a session tree; an agent TUI needs lineage for agent-pin attribution; a durable engine needs history to survive crashes.

**Failure propagation.** conc has three failure channels kept distinct: rethrown panic via `Catcher.Try` + `Repanic` carrying `runtime.Callers`+`debug.Stack` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/panics/panics.go:52-60`), joined errors via `errors.Join`, and context cancellation (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:26-47`). Temporal collapses panics into a typed `workflowPanicError` that fails the workflow deterministically (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:1234-1245`) — never isolated to a sibling. docker-agent has no child panic recovery at all; `HandleRun` has no `recover` and a panic would leave `status=running` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:356`). Same dimension, opposite tradeoffs: library precision, engine durability, product gap.

**Bounded parallelism shape.** conc: semaphore channel that blocks the submitter (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:54-66`); temporal: `Semaphore.Acquire` that blocks via `Await` inside a coroutine (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow.go:2045-2054`) — true queueing semantics under deterministic scheduling; docker-agent: admission control returning a tool error (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:319-329`) — rejection, not queueing. `errgroup.WithContext`+`SetLimit` for children exists nowhere in docker-agent; for RAG it does (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/rag/embed/embed.go:120-122`). The distinction is whether boundedness is push-back (block) or refusal (error) — products that talk to LLMs often prefer refusal with visible tool output over silently blocking the tool dispatch loop.

**Result ordering guarantee.** conc: deterministic submission order by construction, tested with random sleeps (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool_test.go:66-84`). temporal: caller-defined — ordered if you iterate futures sequentially, completion-ordered if you `append` inside `Go`, Selector-ordered otherwise; no helper enforces one. docker-agent: non-deterministic for background — `concurrent.Map.Range` order (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:448-456`); single-value collapse for forwarding (`GetLastAssistantMessageContent()` at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:411`). This maps to maturity: libraries can guarantee ordering cheaply; platforms/platforms-abstracted runtimes leave it to callers until enough callers hurt.

**Terminal reusability vs terminal-once.** conc pools reset `initOnce`/`errs`/`agg` after `Wait` for reuse (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:79`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/error_pool.go:38`), matching `sync.WaitGroup` semantics but diverging from Aren's expected terminal-once parent. docker-agent has no pool to reuse but allows re-spawning background tasks indefinitely up to `maxTotalTasks` with `pruneCompleted()`. temporal is terminal-once per workflow execution — `isWorkflowCompleted` guards re-entry (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow_testsuite.go:1143-1147`) — though the local dispatcher is torn down via `Close`+`Goexit` rather than reused.

**Context ownership.** conc's `Pool.WithContext` creates and owns a derived `context.WithCancel` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:140-148`); `ContextPool.Wait` defers `cancel` even on panic (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:54-58`) so sibling lifetime belongs to the pool. temporal's `cancelCtx` mirrors stdlib propagation but under the deterministic dispatcher (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/context.go:181-325`) plus an escape hatch `NewDisconnectedContext` for cleanup (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/context.go:197-200`). docker-agent's background path explicitly escapes the tree via `context.WithoutCancel` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`), so ownership is severed. Three answers to "who kills children when?" — owner-cancels, dispatcher-cancels, no-one (leaked until `StopAll`).

## Tradeoffs

**Explicit cancellation policy vs implicit fail-fast.** conc requires `WithCancelOnError` to make sibling cancellation happen (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:78-82`); without it, `ErrorPool` merely `errors.Join`s. Benefit: auditable, avoids surprise `context.Canceled` noise in `errors.Join` — tests verify both branches. Cost: callers expecting fail-fast by default must remember the flag, and `ErrorPool` without context has no sibling-cancel path at all. Alternative: docker-agent and temporal default to isolated — one background task failure does not touch siblings (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:412`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow.go:1400-1405`). Best-fit context: libraries serving diverse callers should default isolated and require explicit opt-in to cancellation; engines where workflows orchestrate transactions may want fail-fast default but temporal deliberately left it explicit to avoid hidden leaks under `ABANDON`.

**First-error vs collect-all error join.** Default `errors.Join` keeps every child's failure for debugging; `WithFirstError` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/error_pool.go:66-70`) is deterministic for `WithCancelOnError` where trailing errors would be `context.Canceled` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool_test.go:192-205`). But "first" depends on completion timing — tests acknowledge it is racy and need 10 ms sleeps to make it likely deterministic (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool_test.go:175-184`). Failure mode: choosing `WithFirstError` when diagnostics matter silently discards failures.

**Blocking backpressure vs admission error.** conc's blocking `Go` on `limiter <- struct{}` prevents OOM from unbounded submission but stalls the submitter at `MaxGoroutines=1`. Benefit: no dropped work, no queue to manage. Cost: liveness coupling between producer and pool; no `TryGo`/ctx-aware variant for plain `Pool`. Alternative seen in docker-agent: check caps then return a tool error visible to the LLM (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:319-329`) — suitable when losing or delaying a tool call is preferable to blocking the only dispatch loop. An Aren agent loop that feeds children from LLM tool calls likely wants docker-agent's rejection model for outer scheduling and conc's blocking model for inner worker pools.

**First panic vs all panics.** conc's `Catcher.CompareAndSwap(nil, &Recovered)` keeps only the first panic (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/panics/panics.go:29`); temporal similarly captures one `workflowPanicError` per dispatcher. Benefit: deterministic re-panic value. Cost: subsequent stacks discarded despite "all tasks still execute" guarantee (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:30-32`); simultaneous panics lose diagnostics. Keeping a slice of recovered panics (suggested in conc's future considerations) trades determinism of value for completeness of signal.

**Per-task channel vs global bus economics.** `Stream` per-`Go` `getCh()`/`putCh()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:66-69`) and `resultAggregator` slot isolation give precise attribution without a global bus, but add per-task allocation requiring `sync.Pool` mitigation (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:142-154`). docker-agent's `SessionScoped` filtering keeps the parent transcript clean by dropping mismatched events (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/persistence_observer.go:78-79`) at the cost of losing incremental child observability (only `SubSessionCompleted`/`TokenUsage` survive).

**Reuse vs terminal-once.** Resetting `initOnce`/`errs`/`agg` reduces allocation and matches `sync.WaitGroup` ergonomics. But a `Pool` that silently resurrects workers when a task captures it and calls `Go` after `Wait` violates the dimension's "can a child outlive/mutate after parent is terminal?" test — there is no `atomic.Bool` terminal guard. Temporal's terminal-once dispatch plus policy-driven outlive (`ABANDON` lets child server-persist after parent `Complete`) shows reuse and outlive are separate axes: pools should be reusable *or* terminal, not both, without explicit generational fencing.

## Decision Guide

**Choose the composition shape by answering three questions:**

1. **Is parent/child identity hierarchical?** If fan-out depth >1, cross-session attribution, or agent pinning matter — adopt docker-agent's `ParentID`+`DelegationLineage` with `validateDelegation`-style clone guards (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:60-85`). If execution is a single flat fan-out inside one request, conc's slot index as lightweight `ChildID` plus mutex reservation suffices.

2. **Must children queue or must they be refused?** "Run 5 helpers, at most 3 concurrent, keep all results" — blocking `Pool.Go` with `WithMaxGoroutines` (queueing) is conc's pattern. "LLM asked for 20 background agents, reject over-budget" — admission-error with `maxConcurrentTasks`/`maxTotalTasks` is docker-agent's pattern. For Aren: outer agent loop likely needs refusal visible as a tool error; inner execution helpers likely need queueing, so both belong at different layers.

3. **Fail-fast after one child fails, or collect-all?** Ask whether siblings remain useful without the failed sibling. Parallel fetches of independent evidence → collect-all (`errors.Join`, submission-ordered results). Parallel subagents where one success unlocks the next step or resource is scarce → fail-fast (`WithCancelOnError`/`WithFailFast`) with `WithFirstError` to avoid `Canceled` noise. Temporal leaves this to caller — decide explicitly per composition site and expose as `SubSessionConfig.FailFast bool` (as docker-agent's future considerations suggest).

**When to borrow from each source:**

- Borrow conc's `ResultPool[T]` + `resultAggregator` verbatim for any typed gather — generic result collection without erasure is the most reusable primitive in this dimension.
- Borrow conc's `WithCancelOnError` naming and `addErr`-before-`cancel` discipline for any context-aware pool — make cancellation a configuration time choice, not a doc comment.
- Borrow temporal's dual future + `ParentClosePolicy`/`WaitForCancellation` separation for durable or long-lived children — start confirmation and final result need distinct handles, and "can children outlive parent?" must be a policy flag not an implementation accident.
- Borrow docker-agent's lineage snapshot test (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation_test.go:744-754`) and `swapCurrentAgent` isolation pattern when delegated children carry executor affinity (tool sets, agent names, permissions).
- Borrow temporal's `Semaphore` bounding approach when scheduling is cooperative — `Await(size-cur>=n)` is cancellation-aware and replay-safe, unlike a raw `chan struct{}` limiter that would block the dispatcher.

**Do not borrow yet:** `context.WithoutCancel` detachment for background work until Aren defines whether background children may outlive the parent stream — docker-agent's leak-until-`StopAll` model is the least structured option studied. Likewise, avoid `Pool` reusability after `Wait` without a terminal guard; if Aren needs pool reuse, fence it behind an explicit generational token.

## Practical Tips

1. **Index-reserve before goroutine start.** Copy `resultAggregator.nextIndex()` — mutex-guarded increment before dispatch (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:96-103`) — into any typed gather you build. The pattern is ~15 LOC, races only at submission, and buys submission-order guarantees that no post-hoc sort can recover once values are collected out of order.

2. **Keep fail-fast and first-error distinct combinators.** Expose `WithCancelOnError` and `WithFirstError` separately and provide `WithFailFast` as the composition of both (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:87-92`). Callers who need first-error ordering (trailing `Canceled` noise) get it; callers who need full diagnostics keep `errors.Join`. Test both branches as conc does: assert joined errors lack/ contain `context.Canceled`.

3. **Make boundedness a construction guard, not a runtime tweak.** Follow `panicIfInitialized` — panic if `WithMaxGoroutines` arrives after `tasks != nil` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:108-115`). This prevents mid-flight limiter changes that would require draining semantics and keeps `deref`-copy correctness obvious. Reset only after a terminal `Wait` that drains `range tasks`.

4. **Send empty callbacks before re-panicking in ordered streams.** When a task panics inside `Stream`, send `func(){}` to the `callbackCh` before re-panic (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:77-79`) so the single `callbacker`'s `<-callbackCh` does not starve. Isolate each callback in `panicCatcher.Try` + `Repanic` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/stream/stream.go:121-138`). Copy this bracketing for any ordered-callback fan-out.

5. **Own the derived context in the pool, cancel with defer.** Create `context.WithCancel` in `WithContext` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:140-148`) and `defer cancel` in `Wait` even on panic (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:54-58`). This ties sibling lifetime to the composition handle, not to whichever caller context happened to be passed, and prevents context leaks without relying on caller discipline.

6. **Clone lineage/slice snapshots eagerly for fan-out.** Follow `validateDelegation`'s `append(parent.LineageSnapshot(), caller)` into a freshly allocated slice (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:70`) — the concurrent pinned-transfer isolation test proves sharing a backing array lets one branch mutate the other's view (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation_test.go:1087-1190`).

7. **Use session-scoped sinks instead of a global bus.** Tag events with `GetSessionID()` and filter at the observer (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/event.go:18-22`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/persistence_observer.go:74-79`). Route exceptional child signals (e.g., background TokenUsage) through a dedicated `OnBackgroundEvent` sink (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/runtime.go:179-184`) rather than overloading the parent channel.

8. **Defer `SubagentStop` against the parent executor.** Both `runForwarding` and `runCollecting` defer `executeSubagentStopHooks` against the *parent* agent's executor even on `ErrorEvent`/`ctx.Done()` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:372-380`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:446-448`). This makes completion observability unconditional — copy for any hook that must fire regardless of success/failure.

9. **Budget-share explicitly.** Install one `budgetSet` via `ensureBudget()` guarded by `budgetMu` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/loop.go:240-244`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/runtime.go:324-328`), then have sub-sessions reach that same budget. Document whether UI aggregates by session-ID or globally to avoid double-count on `BudgetUsage` events.

10. **Add `ChildID` handles early, even for in-process pools.** `resultAggregator.nextIndex` already yields the primitive (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_pool.go:96-103`). Wrap it as a typed `ChildHandle` returned by `Go()` so callers can cancel/signal by ID without a separate `taskID` registry.

## Anti-Patterns / Caution Signs

- **Silently reusable pools without a terminal guard.** `Pool.Wait` resets `initOnce`/`errs`/`agg` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:79`) so a task capturing the pool can resurrect workers after the parent appears terminal — the dimension's core failure mode "child mutates after parent is terminal" becomes possible by capture. Caution sign: `Go` does not reject after `Wait` completed. Fix: add an `atomic.Bool` terminal latch that rejects `Go` post-terminal; make reuse opt-in via a new pool instance.

- **Background children detached by `WithoutCancel` without a parent barrier.** `taskCtx` from `context.WithoutCancel(ctx)` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:337`) lets `runCollecting` mutations (`AddLiveSubSession`, `emitBackgroundEvent`, `persistBackgroundSubSession` at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:498-521`) run after `loop.go:201-211` `finalizeEventChannel` emits `StreamStopped` and closes the parent channel. Caution sign: parent terminal is `StreamStopped`+`close(events)` while children still hold `sess.mu` and `session.Store` references; only global `StopAll` at `LocalRuntime.Close()` stops them. If Aren adopts detached work, require a `WaitForChildren` barrier or per-parent `StopAll` before close.

- **No panic recovery in child execution paths.** `pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:356` has no `recover`; a single tool panic crashes the handler goroutine and leaves `task` in `running` forever while `StopAll` waits via `wg.Wait()`. Caution sign: grep for `recover()` hits only channel guards (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/event_sink.go:36`), not child bodies. Require `defer recover()->storeStatus(taskFailed)` for every child goroutine.

- **Completion-order append masquerading as ordered results.** `results = append(results, result)` inside `WaitGroup.Go` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/internal_workflow_test.go:1101-1103`) is ordered by cooperative dispatch timing, not submission — safe only under `dispatcherImpl`'s single-threaded `ExecuteUntilAllBlocked`, not under real goroutines, and undocumented. Caution sign: collection without an index key. Replace with slot-reservation.

- **Unordered map iteration presented as result enumeration.** `HandleList` `Range` over `concurrent.Map` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:448-456`) with `formatView` printing unsorted (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/tools/builtin/agent/agent.go:178-234`) pushes ordering responsibility nowhere. Caution sign: "list all background results" with random order; fix by storing `submissionOrder` and sorting `HandleList`.

- **Racy first-error ordering patched with sleeps.** Tests acknowledge scheduler dependence and insert 10 ms sleeps to make first-error likely correct (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool_test.go:175-184`). Caution sign: `time.Sleep` guarding an ordering assertion — document as scheduler-best-effort or switch to sorted-join, not deterministic first.

- **`errors.Join` ordering is completion order.** `ErrorPool` `errors.Join(errs...)` order (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/error_pool.go:46`) is not submission order; simultaneous child failure ordering cannot be asserted deterministically. If error position matters, key errors by `ChildID`.

- **`WithFirstError` + concurrent `addErr`/`cancel` race.** Without `addErr`-before-`cancel` layering (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/context_pool.go:40-43`), a trailing `context.Canceled` could beat the causal error under `WithFailFast` — a leaky-abstraction break the source deliberately annotates.

- **Reconfiguration after `Go` panics instead of returning error.** `WithMaxGoroutines(0)` or any `With*` after first `Go` panics via `panicIfInitialized` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:93-94`, `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/error_pool.go:90-92`) rather than surfacing a composable error — calling code that builds pools dynamically must order configuration before submissions or crash.

## Notable Absences

Evidence the sources do **not** provide, relevant to Aren Phase 12 scoping:

- **Hierarchical cancellation propagation.** conc has no parent→child→grandchild cancellation tree; docker-agent backgrounds sever it; temporal has the tree (`cancelCtx` children map at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/context.go:181-325`) but tests focus on single-level `WaitGroup` collect-all, not cascade-cancel of a child subtree. Aren will need to design hierarchical `cancelCtx`-like propagation explicitly if sub-agents can spawn their own sub-agents.

- **Parent terminal guard rejecting late children.** None of the sources enforces "Go after Wait is an error" structurally: conc reuses, docker-agent admits new background tasks until caps, temporal's dispatcher `Goexit`s pre-existing coroutines but `ExecuteChildWorkflow` post-`Complete` is a workflow-level history concern, not an in-process panic. `atomic.Bool` terminal flag after `Wait`/`Complete` is a proposed addition in conc's future considerations but absent in the code.

- **Bounded-pool queueing with context-aware submission.** conc's `Pool.Go` always blocks on `limiter`/`tasks` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/pool.go:54-66`) with no `GoContext(ctx)`/`TryGo`; docker-agent has caps but no semaphore queue; temporal has `Semaphore` but no `Pool(n)` wrapper. A bounded composition helper that is both cancellation-aware and type-preserving does not exist in any source.

- **Built-in ordered failure collection.** conc provides ordered *result* collection but not ordered *error* collection; `errors.Join` is completion-ordered and errored slot filtering is invisible by default (`WithCollectErrored=false` drops `T` for failed indices at `studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/conc/pool/result_error_pool.go:35-39`). docker-agent and temporal have neither ordered success nor ordered failure helpers.

- **Explicit child resource-cleanup hooks per parent.** None of the sources shows per-child `Cleanup` callbacks registered with the parent to run after that child's goroutine exits but before `Wait` returns, beyond `context cancel` — child cleanup completion relies entirely on goroutine exit, not a `defer`-like contract. Temporal's `WaitForCancellation` (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/temporal-sdk-go/internal/workflow.go:484-487`) is the closest, gating whether parent waits for child's cancel confirmation, but it is not a general cleanup mechanism.

- **Simultaneous child failure + panic integration test.** conc tests panic isolation and isolated sibling cancellation but not "two children fail at once, assert sibling cancel policy + error join shape + panic rank"; docker-agent has no simultaneous background-failure test ("no evidence of simultaneous child failure test" per gaps); temporal's `waitGroupWorkflowTest` sums results without exercising fail-fast policy. Aren should port conc's random-sleep order test and add a deterministic simultaneous-failure test covering `WithFailFast` vs `CollectAll` + `WithCollectErrored`.

## Per-Source Notes

Brief anchors; the synthesis above carries the comparative weight.

- **conc (6/10):** Flat scoped-concurrency library, stdlib-only. Most complete set of Phase 12 mechanics studied: lazy bounded-pool workers, typed `Pool→ResultPool[T]` composition that preserves limiter semantics, submission-ordered `resultAggregator`, explicit opt-in sibling cancellation with tested `WaitFirstError` vs `JoinAll` branches, panic capture with `Recovered` stacks, single-threaded ordered `Stream`. Weakest on ownership modeling — no child identity, no hierarchy, no terminal flag beyond barrier, pools reusable after `Wait`. Best template for the composition helpers Arena should actually build.

- **docker-agent (5/10):** Long-lived agent application, bubble-tea TUI + MCP. Composition is product-shaped, not library-shaped: `SubSession` identity (`ParentID`/`DelegationLineage`/depth 10/cycle detection), strictly sequential `runForwarding` with `swapCurrentAgent` and per-session event filtering, and detached `runCollecting` background agents with admission caps (`20/100`) and `WithoutCancel` outlive. No generic pool/gather/fail-fast and no panic recovery. Strongest lesson is concrete executor preservation and lineage isolation; weakest on generalizability — Phase 12 needs a separate generic Group/Pool layer to cover "run 5, limit 3, collect ordered, fail-fast".

- **temporal-sdk-go (6/10):** Durable execution platform. Ownership is durable (server `ParentClosePolicy ABANDON/TERMINATE/REQUEST_CANCEL` + local `dispatcherImpl` coroutines), with clean `Future`/`Channel`/`Selector`/`WaitGroup`/`Semaphore` primitives and separated execution/result futures. No opinionated Group/Pool — bounded parallelism, fail-fast, and ordered gather are left to callers, and tests demonstrate the unsafe patterns (completion-order append). The pattern to copy is `ParentClosePolicy` + `WaitForCancellation` as the explicit answer to "can a child outlive/mutate after parent is terminal?" — yes, only when the policy says ABANDON — and `NewDisconnectedContext` for cleanup that must survive parent cancel.

## Open Questions

1. **Queueing or rejection for bounded parallelism?** conc blocks the submitter; docker-agent returns a tool-visible error; temporal blocks via `Await` under cooperative scheduling. For Aren's agent loop that submits subagents from LLM tool calls, which site should block and which should refuse? Is `GoContext(ctx)` / `TryGo` needed alongside blocking `Go`?

2. **Submission vs completion vs start ordering for results?** conc commits to submission order; temporal and docker-agent leave it undefined. If Aren's evidence view aggregates parallel tool calls, should results be ordered by LLM call index (submission), dispatch start, or actual completion? Should the ordered-gather API return `[]T` in submission order and a separate `[]ChildEvent` in completion order?

3. **Terminal reusability contract.** conc resets after `Wait`; docker-agent caps but never reuses a single handle; temporal is terminal-once per execution. Does Aren want parent-session reuse across generations or terminal-once with explicit new-session spawn? If reuse, how to prevent post-terminal capture-`Go`?

4. **Where do `ParentClosePolicy`-like semantics belong?** docker-agent makes "can a child outlive a parent?" an unchecked consequence of `WithoutCancel`; temporal makes it a typed option on `ChildWorkflowOptions`. Should Aren's subagent launch expose `OutliveParent bool` / `WaitForCancellation` / `BudgetIsolation` per child, or per parent?

5. **Panic isolation boundary.** conc and temporal isolate or convert panics deterministically; docker-agent crashes the handler goroutine. Should child panic be surfaced as `ChildFailed(PanicError)` to siblings via `errors.Join` / fail-fast policy, or always fatal to the parent workflow?

6. **Bounded-pool type shape.** Should Aren's pool be `Group.Equal(Run)`-style that is terminal-once, or a reusable `Pool` like conc? Should the `WithMaxGoroutines` limiter be a channel or `semaphore.Weighted` / temporal-style `Await` — i.e., must it be `ctx`-aware and support `TryAcquire`?

7. **Missing simultaneous-failure tests.** No source asserts the full matrix: two children fail concurrently, verify sibling cancellation (or lack), error join shape, panic rank, and ordered-result filtering. What is the assertion standard for non-deterministic `WithFirstError` — allow either first by completion time, or pin with deterministic sleeps?

8. **Capabilities/permission inheritance scope.** docker-agent's `SubSessionConfig` clones `Permissions`/`SafetyPolicy`/`AllowedTools` per child (`studies/aren-go-runtime-study/sources/docker-agent/pkg/runtime/agent_delegation.go:132-168`). If Aren subagents carry scoped permissions or evidence quotas, does inheritance propagate down the lineage, and does narrowing at depth >1 relax or tighten?
